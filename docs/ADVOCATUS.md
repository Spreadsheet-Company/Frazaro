# ADVOCATUS — the devil's advocate, retained against the premises

*The second execution of SD-17: the advocate's seat, occupied 2026-08-31 —
the same day as the coroner's, a cadence the register below is obliged to
pay for, since two reviews in one sitting is exactly the weather
`PREMORTEM.md` D.7 forecasts. The persona is the oldest professional
skeptic on record: the* advocatus diaboli, *the office Rome created in
1587 because it had noticed that unopposed cases rot. The promoter of the
faith argued against every proposed saint — professionally, whether he
believed the case or not — and the office's one modern datum is the
strongest argument for this document: it was effectively abolished in
1983, and canonizations promptly multiplied by an order of magnitude.
Remove the paid skeptic and the saints get cheaper. This shelf proposes
its share of saints.*

*Sources read in full: `BETA_ROADMAP1.md`, `BETA_ROADMAP2.md`,
`CONSULTANT.md`, `AUDIT.md`, `REBUILD.md`, `LESSONS.md`, `TRENCHES.md`,
`PROJECT_BRIEF.md`, `PREMORTEM.md`, `METAMETALISP4.md`.*

*Scope, and why this persona is not a repeat: every review before this one
audited the work — `CONSULTANT.md` its completeness, `AUDIT.md` its
doctrine, `REBUILD.md` its shape, `PREMORTEM.md` its mortality. All four
took the premises as given, because that is what made them tractable. This
document audits the premises themselves: the sentences so load-bearing
they appear in no ⬜ item, because no item could contain them — they are
what the items stand on. Findings are numbered `A.*`, advisory per
`ID_REGISTRY.md`'s own distinction, never governed.*

*Method, fixed per premise, and one rule above the method: the advocate
argues each negation **to win** — a steelman with dates, not a courtesy
objection — and then names the evidence that would settle the question
and **who must produce it**, which is never this document. An advocate
who manufactures his own evidence is a prosecutor, and the office was
never that.*

**Format, fixed for every premise:** the **premise, as held** (where the
shelf states it) · the **case against, argued to win** · **what the shelf
already concedes** · the **evidence that settles it**, with its producer
named · the **verdict standard** (what would count as the advocate
winning — stated in advance, so a loss cannot be renegotiated into a
draw).

## A.1 — "Checked English beats free English"

**The premise, as held.** The mission paragraph itself; SD-16's wall
("a Frazaro program means one thing, and the loader can prove it");
SIG.2's determinism pitch. The deepest sentence on the shelf: whatever
falls, this falling takes everything with it.

**The case against, argued to win.** History keeps a graveyard for this
exact premise, and the headstones are legible. COBOL promised
English-readable business logic in 1959 and produced a priesthood with a
new dialect to be initiated into. AppleScript proved the fatal asymmetry
in its purest form: a language that *reads* like English but must be
*written* like an argument with a compiler, so that every user's first
sentence fails — and the English-likeness makes each failure more
insulting, not less, because the user wrote English and was told it
wasn't. Inform 7, the best constrained English ever shipped, holds a
devoted niche of authors, not a population of non-programmers. The
pattern generalizes: English-shaped syntax raises expectations to
natural-language level, then bills the difference in refusals. Frazaro's
teaching refusals are the mitigation — and also the confession: a
product that needs this much apparatus to explain why your English
wasn't English is fighting the asymmetry, not exempt from it. And since
2023 the competing bid is not VBA. It is a prompt box that accepts
*anything*, wrongly but instantly. The premise no longer has to beat
harder syntax; it has to beat **zero syntax**, and it has to do so with
users who have already been taught by the prompt box that syntax is a
thing computers no longer require of them.

**What the shelf already concedes.** More than it advertises: LX.8's
never-blame style guide exists because refusals sting; the Phrases sheet
and Known Sentences button exist because the blank first row is a known
cliff (`CONSULTANT.md` §2.10); `AUDIT.md` Part II asked the readability
question out loud; SIG.2 and LE.7 name Copilot by name. The apparatus of
concession is excellent. It is still apparatus.

**The evidence that settles it.** PI.2 — the pilot's SOP in their own
words, verbatim, *before* any grammar work — diffed against the corpus.
The measured distance between what a real person writes and what the
grammar holds is this premise's price tag, in sentences. Then the
concierge run's curve: does the pilot's third refused sentence land
closer than their first? Producer: the pilot, not the owner, and
emphatically not this document.

**Verdict standard.** The advocate wins if the teaching refusals turn
out to teach the *owner* — who edits the grammar to meet the user —
rather than the *user*, who was supposed to learn from them. That
direction of adaptation, sustained, means the premise is quietly false
and being falsified politely, one grammar patch at a time.

## A.2 — "The owner's ear generalizes"

**The premise, as held.** SD-3's append-only audition checklist;
`LESSONS.md` IV ("the user's ear as a linting pass"); every adjudication
in the ledgers that ends with the owner's ear deciding between
spellings.

**The case against, argued to win.** An audition with one judge produces
an idiolect, however excellent the judge. Every choice between `value in
column B` and its rivals was adjudicated by one ear — and the
adjudications that felt *obvious* are precisely where a second ear would
diverge, because obviousness is what idiolect feels like from the
inside. SD-3 then makes the situation structural: the checklist accretes
the judge's taste and enforces it append-only, so the portrait is not
only painted but varnished. The corpus may be a very good likeness of
one mind's English, mistaken for English.

**What the shelf already concedes.** The phrase "the user's ear as a
linting pass" already files the ear as an instrument, and instruments
expect calibration. LX.10's dialect evidence showed surface translation
is cheap — which cuts the advocate's way and back: cheap to have
overfit, cheap to correct.

**The evidence that settles it.** PI.2 again — the transcript *is* the
second ear, arriving verbatim — plus LX.7's published spec placed in
front of one stranger who must **write** sentences, not admire them.
Producer: a stranger. The defining property of the required witness is
that the owner has never spoken with them about Excel.

**Verdict standard.** The advocate wins if the transcript's phrasings
require per-user grammar surgery rather than teaching-distance nudges —
if meeting the second ear means repainting rather than refocusing.

## A.3 — "The 1:1 bargain stays a feature"

**The premise, as held.** `LESSONS.md` I — the founding bargain, the
compiler trapdoor never opened, "VBA wearing parentheses," every hard
distinction resolved once and hidden downstream.

**The case against, argued to win.** The bargain's ledger has two
columns, and the shelf reads only one aloud. Declining semantic
smoothing avoided the trapdoor — granted, repeatedly, and the discipline
was real. But every VBA sharp edge must then go one of two places:
absorbed into the bucket brigade (which grows monotonically, absorbed
edge by absorbed edge, with no stated asymptote) or passed through to a
user who was promised they would never meet VBA — and who therefore
files VBA's own semantics as Frazaro bugs, because the parenthesis
costume does not indemnify the wearer. And the advocate notes for the
record that the premise has already been breached in the only way that
matters: IN.9 made the interpreter the *default runtime*. Parity with
VBA is now a promise Frazaro maintains with its own tests — which is
ownership of semantics in all but name. The wall held. The city moved.

**What the shelf already concedes.** SD-5's support-or-refuse rule is
the honest tax on the breach; the four-question template test is a
working ratchet with a rejection log; the declined-feature records are
discipline, not decoration.

**The evidence that settles it.** Two counts, both producible by the
repository: where the four-question test's rejections *went* (died,
worked around, or absorbed anyway a version later), and the
absorbed-edge census per version. A monotone climb with no flattening is
the advocate's exhibit A.

**Verdict standard.** The advocate wins on the day a user-facing
behavior exists whose honest documentation must say "because VBA" — to a
user the mission paragraph promised would never need to know what VBA
is.

## A.4 — "Auditability is a differentiator buyers act on"

**The premise, as held.** The pitch `AUDIT.md` Part III made a horizon
of — "the procedure she wrote is the procedure the auditor reads" —
carried by U.18, and standing as the third leg of SIG.2's positioning.

**The case against, argued to win.** Revealed preference. Finance runs
on Excel — the least auditable load-bearing artifact in any building —
and has for forty years, with the world's auditors signing off beside
it annually. Nobody has ever been blocked because their macros were
insufficiently auditable; they are blocked because macros *exist* — the
trust settings, the real gate, which auditability does not open.
"Auditable automation" may be a value compliance officers cite in
surveys and never once purchase against: a differentiator that
differentiates in documents only. The advocate's cruelest phrasing: the
pitch's best evidence to date is how good it sounds on this shelf.

**What the shelf already concedes.** U.18's own text admits the pitch
currently has no artifact behind it. SIG.1 names the actual approval
gate, and it is not this.

**The evidence that settles it.** One practicing reviewer — proposed as
a mint below (candidate `SIG.6`): put SIG.1, when it exists, in front of
a real IT reviewer or auditor and record verbatim what they would block
on. Producer: the reviewer. If auditability appears nowhere in their
answer, the differentiator is a decoration with a department.

**Verdict standard.** As above, and pre-agreed: absence from the
verbatim is a loss. Presence in the verbatim *unprompted* is the
premise's full acquittal, and worth more than this document.

## A.5 — "No network, ever, is survivable"

**The premise, as held.** SD-13 — the best-argued entry in the register,
owner-litigated, with its own re-litigation clause.

**The case against, argued to win.** The posture starves three loops at
once, and loops are the only things that compound. *Security:* SEC.5's
own text names the tension — a disclosed vulnerability has no path to
the users already running the affected build; the product's worst week
is the week this bites, and the posture guarantees the bite arrives
unannounced. *Learning:* a no-telemetry product knows nothing at scale
that a pilot didn't say out loud; the mission's own constraint line
says the bottleneck is learning, and SD-13 caps the learning rate at
the speed of voluntary anecdote. *Support:* SIG.3's incident response
necessarily begins "the user notices," which is to say it begins late.
The pitch sells the absence of a liability. Absences do not compound.
The starved loops do.

**What the shelf already concedes.** SD-13 is well-made even if wrong:
it names its first temptation (DI.3), demands dedicated re-litigation
rather than drift, and PI.7 is already scoped to read what the one IT
department that matters actually permits. The advocate's respect for the
entry's craftsmanship is genuine and changes nothing about the loops.

**The evidence that settles it.** Two items. PI.7's trust reading: is
no-network actually *decisive* for that IT department, or merely
pleasant — a selling point nobody would have blocked without? And a
written answer to "how does a fix reach an affected user," produced
**before** the first CVE rather than by it. The answer may well be
SD-13-compatible — a user-initiated check, a release-page habit, a
signed hash list — but it must exist as a sentence, not a shrug.
Producer: the owner, on the calendar's schedule, not the attacker's.

**Verdict standard.** The advocate wins if the CVE question has no
SD-13-compatible answer — in which case the remedy is not this
document's opinion but SD-13's own re-litigation clause, honored to the
letter it was written with.

## A.6 — "Solo plus assistant reaches industry-grade"

**The premise, as held.** The collaboration protocol entire; the shelf's
existence; `LESSONS.md` X's claim that the method was the real product
all along.

**The case against, argued to win.** Read the protocol's own ledger as a
hostile actuary would: roughly twenty bugs caught only at the keyboard,
by the one human whose evenings are the QA lab; blind authorship as a
standing condition; session amnesia so structural it required a
campaign-history literature to compensate. Every discipline on this
shelf is a prosthesis for a redundancy the model lacks — the goldens for
memory, the checkmark rule for overclaiming, the topology drills for
the dev environment's flattery — and the docs' celebrated excellence is
partly a symptom: this much prosthetic memory implies this much
amputation. Industry-grade, wherever it is actually practiced, *means*
redundant human eyes. Here the second eye resets between sittings and
the first eye is also the compiler, the runtime, and the market.

**What the shelf already concedes.** The prostheses are load-bearing and
documented — which industry mostly is not. Goldens, self-tests, and
topology discipline are real redundancy, mechanical where it cannot be
human. The defect-escape record exists *as a record*, which is more than
most funded teams can produce about themselves.

**The evidence that settles it.** The defect-escape rate per version —
bugs found live that the suite had passed — trending against corpus
growth; and `CN.1`'s drill (the coroner's mint, one review ago) as the
transfer test the protocol has never once been forced to pass.
Producer: the repository, counted; and one stranger at a clean machine.

**Verdict standard.** The advocate wins if the escape rate holds flat
while the corpus grows — the compensations compounding slower than the
surface they compensate for. That is the honest arithmetic test of
whether the method scales or merely holds.

  A.6. *(Depends on: SIG.1.)*
- **Proposed reading, not a mint — PI.2 as the falsification

  the same evidence: the pilot's verbatim transcript and what happens
  when the corpus meets it. The advocate proposes no new item and one
  relabeling: PI.2 is not a pilot step that also yields data — it is
  **the single cheapest experiment on this shelf that can kill or crown
  the mission premise**, and it should be read, scheduled, and
  protected as such.
- **Killed — nothing,** for stated reasons rather than politeness: an
  advocate who wins a kill on a *premise* is not trimming the roadmap,
  he is closing the project, and no verdict above has its evidence in
  yet. The office's job was never to win. It was to make winning mean
  something.

## How this advocate could be wrong

- **Arguing negations selects for arguable negations.** The fatal
  premise may be one both sides share so completely neither can see
  around it. The candidate this advocate can name but not argue:
  **"Excel is where the users are."** Every document on this shelf,
  this one included, assumes it. Filed for the platform historian
  (SUBSTRATE's persona), who is the only seat positioned to doubt it.
- **The canonization frame biases toward acquittal.** The office
  existed to be overcome — its arguments were institutionally destined
  to lose — and a document shaped like the office may inherit the
  outcome along with the shape.
- **The graveyard in A.1 is survivorship-read.** COBOL also *won* its
  era, colossally, on exactly the readable-business-logic pitch; Inform
  7's niche may be the size of interactive fiction, not the ceiling of
  constrained English. Headstones make poor base rates.
- **A.6's evidence cuts at its own reviewer.** The advocate is written
  inside the dyad he cross-examines — see Ω, where this is not
  resolved, only disclosed.
- **Same-day cadence.** Two SD-17 executions in one sitting is D.7's
  exact weather, and the coroner said so first. If the next fork finds
  these documents unre-read and their registers unadjudicated, the
  correct reading is that D.7 claimed them both, this paragraph
  included.

## Ω — the advocate's conflict of interest

He is on retainer to the saint's family. Written by the same hands that
wrote the case for canonization, in the same sitting, in the house style
the case was written in — and the style is the tell: a genuinely outside
advocate would not have gotten the epigraph jokes. The office's
structural answer, the only honest one available, is the evidence rule
enforced above: every verdict names evidence that someone *else* must
produce — a pilot's transcript, a stranger's writing hand, a reviewer's
verbatim, a rate the repository counts whether anyone likes it or not.
The advocate's opinions are as conflicted as his employment; his verdict
standards are not, because they were published in advance and assigned
to other witnesses. An advocate who cannot be wrong is a mood with a
cassock. This one can be wrong six named ways, and has filed where each
would show.

The seat is now occupied and the persona ledger advances: per SD-17,
the successor, the accountant, and the platform historian remain — and
the historian should begin with the premise both parties above agreed
not to see.

*Filed the same day as the coroner's report, against the advice implicit
in it, and in partial payment of the debt it names.*

*End of cross-examination.*
