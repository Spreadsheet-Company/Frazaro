## N.1 — The betting arithmetic has an undeclared currency

**The line item.** The roadmap's own ordering rule, stated at every
fork: *cost-of-delay ÷ appetite.* A genuinely good fraction — and a
fraction from finance, where cost-of-delay has a definition, and the
definition requires a **value function**: cost of delay *of what*,
denominated *in what*. The shelf never says. Every `~hours` and
`~weeks` on the roadmap prices the denominator with care; the numerator
runs on an unstated conversion rate.

**The books as kept.** In practice the implicit currency has been
*mission progress plus learning* — visible in every adjudication: the
constraint line names learning as the bottleneck, SD-12 privileges the
work that produces specimens, and the betting table's revealed
preference is corpus over commerce every time. This is a coherent
currency. It is also a **choice**, and an undeclared choice cannot be
audited, re-litigated, or even noticed when it silently changes.

**The correction, made concrete with one real item.** Take GO.4 —
licensing split, CLA, trademark, `~weeks`, currently sitting unranked
in the Commons tranche. Under one destiny below it is near-worthless
ceremony; under another it is the founding act; under a third it is the
single item whose absence unwinds the entire endgame. Same item, same
`~weeks`, three different costs-of-delay — because cost-of-delay is a
function of destiny, and the shelf computes it without one. The
arithmetic is not wrong. It is *incomplete in a way that has been
harmless only because every destiny agrees about the near term* — and
the near term is exactly the part that is ending at beta.

**Cost to fix.** Zero new arithmetic; one declaration. Subsumed by
N.5's mint.

## N.2 — Five destinies, one roadmap, no declared bet

**The line item.** What does winning look like, in units other than
checkmarks? No document on the shelf answers, and the mission paragraph
— sincerely held — is a *direction*, not a destination. The accountant
finds five destinations implicit in the shelf itself, each already
paying items on the roadmap without ever being named as their payer.

| Destiny | The win, in one sentence | First irreversible commitment | Characteristic death |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Gift** | Strangers use and extend it, free | The license (GO.4) | D.2 — gifts still need recipients |
| **The Lifestyle Product** | Dozens of seats sustain the evenings | The price list (SD-10's own "hardest thing to un-publish") | D.0/D.1 — churn by paper cut; the funder is also the staff |
| **The Instrument** | The method and shelf as the proof-of-work | Declaring it (see below) | None — which is the warning |
| **The Dowry** | An acquirer values the corpus and method | Entangling contributions without a CLA (GO.4 again) | D.5 — commodification zeroes the price before the sale |
| **The Register** | The telos: a governed core, community editions | Freezing the core's semantics publicly (SD-4 at standard scale) | D.1 — constitutions take decades; the court has one justice |

**The books as kept, per destiny — briefly, because the shelf already
wrote most of each without filing them:**

- **The Gift.** GO.2–GO.5 and DO.4 are this destiny's items; its
  hinge is that copyleft-versus-permissive decides whether the
  phrasebook ecosystem becomes a commons or a market — which promotes
  GO.4 from `~weeks` of afterthought to the founding act. Everything
  SIG demotes except what adoption inside companies still needs.
- **The Lifestyle Product.** The honest arithmetic nobody has run: at
  solo support capacity, support load caps the customer count, so the
  price must make *dozens* sufficient — which selects the buyer
  (departments and small firms, not consumers) and thereby re-ranks
  SIG.\* and SIG.3 to the top of the working shelf. SD-10, read
  closely, is this destiny's constitution already: *price seats,
  support, the registry, governance, hosted services — never
  semantics* is an open-core business model with the name redacted.
  The register wrote the pricing constraint before the pricing
  question.
- **The Instrument.** Legitimate, common, and the only destiny
  reachable **by accident**: the shelf-as-product retroactively
  justifies every hour ever spent on the shelf, which is why it must
  be chosen in daylight or not at all. Its unique property: it is the
  one destiny D.7 cannot kill — documentation death is documentation
  *victory* here — and a defense that strong should never be adopted
  by drift.
- **The Dowry.** Rarely aimed at, worth naming because its
  prerequisites are cheap and shared: clean chain of title (sole
  authorship makes it clean today; SIG.0 makes it *documented*), the
  competitive one-pager as prospectus, and pilot evidence as the
  demand exhibit. Its hinge is again GO.4 — the same `~weeks` item is
  the pivot of two destinies and dead weight in a third, which is N.1's
  finding wearing a specific ID.
- **The Register.** The telos, in `CONTEMPLATIONS.md`'s own words: a
  fixed semantic core governed like a constitution, editions curated
  like dictionaries, org and user layers a living register — programs
  stored as meaning, rendered as wording. The longest horizon on the
  shelf, the destiny the mission is actually denominated in, and the
  one §8 warns needs runway above all: THE TWO NEUTRALITIES and GO.\*
  entire are its items, and its characteristic death is the funder's
  withdrawal, because no one else can inherit a constitution that was
  never declared.

**The correction.** Not a choice — a *declaration of the option
structure*: which destinies remain open, which are already closed by
standing decisions (SD-10 quietly closed "sell the semantics" years
before anyone asked), and what evidence re-prices them. Destinies
compose (Gift × Lifestyle is exactly SD-10's carve-out list); the point
is not picking one, it is knowing which items are option premiums on
which futures.

**Cost to fix.** One document section, `~hours` — the mint in N.5.

## N.3 — The burn is real and unbooked

**The line item.** The project's true operating cost. The shelf prices
every item in `~hours`/`~days`/`~weeks` and never states whose hours,
at what rate, funded by what. The answers: the owner's; a market rate
the owner could be charging someone; funded by donation — the owner's
evenings, underwritten by joy and mission.

**The books as kept.** Nowhere, and the omission has teeth: an
unbooked burn cannot be noticed *rising* — scope that would alarm a
funded team's controller arrives here as one more tranche. The shelf's
one hedge is accidental: SD-12's grammar-slice floor, which this report
re-books under its true account — **dividend policy.** The grammar
slice is the payment the funder receives each version; it is what keeps
the investment renewed; and `PREMORTEM.md` D.1 — the investor
withdrawing — is what the shelf calls the scenario where the dividends
stopped covering the position.

**The correction.** Not timesheets — the project would be right to
refuse them. One line in the destiny declaration: the funding model is
donated evenings; the funder's terms are joy plus mission; therefore
every destiny's plan must fit inside evenings *indefinitely* or say
when it stops pretending to. (The Lifestyle destiny's support
arithmetic in N.2 is this line applied once.)

**Cost to fix.** A sentence, and the discipline of believing it.

## N.4 — Distribution is unpriced in every destiny

**The line item.** How does user #2 find Frazaro? Every destiny above
— gift, product, dowry, register — requires discovery, and the shelf's
distribution machinery (the DI tranche) is entirely *installation*:
what happens after an artifact has already arrived on a machine.
Upstream of that there is nothing: no README anywhere in the tree, no
CHANGELOG, no hosting item on any roadmap, and `DEPLOY.md`'s download
table annotated "reusable near-verbatim as a future website's copy" —
copy written for a website that exists on no roadmap. The funnel has
been built from the second step down.

**The books as kept.** Deliberately deferred, and defensibly: with
zero external users, discovery has no specimen, and SD-7's spirit says
don't build for a visitor not yet named. But the deferral is nowhere
*written* as a deferral — it is an absence, and absences (the
successor's audit just established) are where this project's real
exposures live. Note also what SD-13 does **not** say: it constrains
the product's outbound calls, not the project's presence; a download
page is not telemetry.

**The correction.** A floor, not a funnel: the repository's front door
— a README that says what this is, in the product's own voice, plus
the CHANGELOG that SD-14's versioning discipline already generates the
raw material for. `~hours`, serves all five destinies identically
(even the Instrument: a portfolio piece with no front door is a
gallery with the lights off), serves G4 — the archaeologist — as a
side effect, and is the single cheapest item this sitting's four
reviews have proposed.

**Cost to fix.** Proposed as `DO.7` below.

## N.5 — The option is real, the premium is being paid, the expiry is unset

**The line item.** The accountant's verdict on the whole question, and
it is *not* "choose now." Deferring the destiny decision is currently
**correct**: the essay's own §8 names unknown requirements as the case
where the constraint is learning, and PI.\* is the instrument that
converts guesses about all five destinies into evidence. Deliberately
unchosen is a real option, and real options are worth money.

**The books as kept.** But an option has a **premium** and an
**expiry**, and the shelf books neither. The premium is being paid
now: appetite spread across items serving different destinies (GO.\*
for the Gift and Register, SIG.\* for the Product and Dowry, the
METAMETA line for the Instrument), each individually justified, the
spread itself never chosen. The expiry does not exist at all — there
is no named event at which the deferral stops being wisdom and starts
being drift, which is the exact shape `AUDIT.md` I.7 flagged in gates
and this shelf has since fixed everywhere *except here*, one level up,
where the whole roadmap is the gate.

**The correction.** Declare the option: one written artifact holding
the destiny table (N.2), the currency declaration (N.1), the funding
line (N.3), and — the part that makes it an instrument instead of an
essay — **the expiry**: the destiny question is re-adjudicated when
the pilot produces its evidence (the gap log, the trust reading, the
first "would you pay" conversation), and at every fork thereafter
until it closes. SIG.5 already does exactly this one level down (the
release's acceptance criteria); this is the same discipline applied to
the project.

**Cost to fix.** `~hours`. The mint below.

## How this report could be wrong

- **The books may be refused, correctly.** A vocation's ledger does
  not balance and should not; the owner may reject the economic frame
  entirely. The finding survives in SD-11's own shape: a refusal
  written down is a decision; a refusal lived silently is a drift —
  and the Instrument destiny's daylight rule applies to refusals too.
- **The five destinies may be a false partition.** They compose
  (open-core is Gift × Lifestyle, and SD-10 already wrote its terms);
  the table is a basis, not a menu, and a hybrid chosen deliberately
  satisfies every finding above.
- **Every number here is shape, not measurement.** Pre-revenue,
  pre-user unit economics are structured guessing; the support-caps-
  customers arithmetic is the report's most confident claim and rests
  on an unmeasured support load. The pilot re-prices this document
  too — which is the strongest argument in it.
- **The receivables warning ages onto its own author.** A fourth
  same-day review is itself an aging receivable, and the accountant
  cannot exempt his own invoice from the collections-day rule. If the
  fork adjudicates none of this, N.5's expiry logic says this report
  was premium paid on an option nobody exercised.
- **The Instrument hazard cuts at this page.** An accountant's report
  is legible, praised-in-standup artifact production — D.7's exact
  weather — and the one destiny this document cannot see past from
  inside is the one where producing documents *is* the win. Filed
  under Ω, where it belongs.

## Ω — the accountant's conflict of interest

The accountant's hours bill to the same donated evenings he just
audited; this report's cost was booked in the currency it exists to
question, and the funder approved the spend the way the funder
approves everything — by finding it interesting. That is the conflict,
and it is not resolvable from this seat: from inside the producing
chair, producing the shelf always feels like work, which is precisely
why the Instrument destiny is the one reachable by accident and the
one this report is least qualified to weigh. The mitigation is the
same one every seat this sitting has used: the verdict is assigned to
evidence someone else produces — the pilot's answers, the fork's
adjudications, the owner's yes said deliberately or not at all.

The persona ledger advances to its last name: the platform historian,
still holding the premise the advocate filed and no seat since has
been positioned to doubt — *"Excel is where the users are."* The
accountant notes in closing that this is also his own largest
unbookable line item: every destiny above is denominated in a
substrate whose total addressable future is exactly what the historian
has not yet been asked about.

*Filed the same sitting as the coroner, the advocate, and the
successor — fourth and cheapest, since by now the shelf's conventions
amortize: the format was inherited, the objections were pre-empted by
colleagues, and the invoice is smaller than the interest on the
findings.*

*End of report.*
