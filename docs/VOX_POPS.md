# VOX POPS — what people might say, before anyone has said it

*Written 2026-09-09 in conversation, filed 2026-09-10. The owner asked for
off-hand, man-on-the-street reactions to one question — "if you could
program spreadsheets in English, what would you do?" — in the register of
guerrilla marketing journalism. Every quotation below is invented, by the
assistant, at that prompt. Nobody said any of them. They are filed anyway,
and in the same spirit as every prediction this project writes down before
a run: they are guesses about what strangers will say, made so that the
real thing can be diffed against them when it arrives.*

*Two rules follow from that. First, none of these may ever be quoted, in
any outward-facing document or post, as if a person said it — the
marketing guardrails against astroturfing apply to imaginary people too.
Second, the day a real person says something in this neighbourhood, their
words replace the guess, verbatim, dated and credited with their consent,
and the guess moves to the retired section at the bottom so the file keeps
its own history. `MARKETING.md`'s standing ask — reply with the sentence
you would have written — is how the real ones arrive.*

## As heard outside a suburban office park, a hospital cafeteria, and one accounting-conference bar

- **Staff accountant, 52.** "I'd type 'close the month' and go home. Then
  I'd come back, because I don't trust it. Then I'd go home again. That's
  two commutes I don't do now."

- **Intern, 22.** "Wait, it isn't already in English? What have I been
  typing?"

- **Owner of a landscaping company.** "'Find the invoice from the guy with
  the truck.' No? Fine. 'Find the invoice from the guy with the *red*
  truck.'"

- **Skeptic in a lanyard.** "Whose English? My manager's English is forty
  percent 'circle back.'"

- **Retiree, walking a dog.** "I had this in 1987. It was called Doreen."

- **Woman who visibly hates Excel.** "I'd write 'delete yourself' and see
  if it has the decency."

- **Regional manager.** "'Make the numbers match.' And I'd pray it doesn't
  ask which ones."

- **FP&A analyst, tired.** "First sentence: 'Undo whatever Kevin did.'
  Second sentence: 'Find out what Kevin did.'"

- **Guy who tried the other robot.** "Copilot wrote me a macro so
  confidently I almost sent the file to the bank. This one says no when it
  doesn't understand? Good. I want the one that says no."

- **Barista who also does the scheduling.** "'Figure out which shifts I can
  cover without dying.' It does that? With the, what, Prolog thing? What's
  Prolog? Never mind. Does it know I can't do Tuesdays?"

- **Internal auditor.** "'Show me who changed cell D14.' Then I'd type it
  again, slower, because nothing has ever answered that question."

- **Software developer, smug.** "So it's COBOL." *It's a Lisp that compiles
  to VBA.* "So it's COBOL with parentheses." *The parentheses are hidden.*
  "So it's COBOL."

- **Man who misheard the question.** "Program it to do what? Like,
  emotionally?"

- **Corporate counsel.** "Can it refuse in writing? I would like the
  refusal in writing."

- **Sales rep.** "'Make Q3 look better.' It refused? Honestly, respect."

- **Excel power user.** "I already talk to Excel in English. Out loud.
  Every day. It has never once listened, so this would mostly be closure."

- **HR coordinator.** "'List everyone who hasn't done the training.' Then,
  'Find out why the list is just me.'"

- **Pirate Day enthusiast.** "'Stow the total in cell B2, arr.' If it
  doesn't take that, I'm out."

- **Charge nurse.** "Every hospital runs on one spreadsheet and one person
  who knows how it works. She's in Cabo. So yes, obviously yes."

- **Data engineer.** "SQL in a cell? I've spent ten years putting cells in
  SQL. It feels like the universe correcting itself."

- **Cynic at the bar.** "Deterministic means it's wrong the same way every
  time. We call that a process. Sign me up."

- **Person holding a MacBook.** "Does it work on Mac?" *Mostly.* "So no."

- **Senior accountant, quietly.** "I'd write down how I actually do the
  close and hand it to whoever replaces me. That's the whole dream. That,
  and not being replaced."

- **CFO, leaving.** "Bring me one page. If the page says 'it refused,' I'll
  read the second page."

## Second round, as heard in a trade-show coffee line, a bank lobby, and at one nonprofit's folding table

*Added 2026-09-10 at the owner's request: the same question, put to other
corporate positions. Same rules as above — invented, never to be quoted as
real, replaced by the first real one that arrives.*

- **IT security administrator.** "So the malware is going to be a
  paragraph now. Fine. I'm adding 'please' to the blocklist."

- **Procurement officer.** "'Refuses to run when it doesn't understand.'
  Finally. A vendor with my department's policy."

- **Controller.** "'Reconcile intercompany.' Then I'd hold my breath. The
  last three people who tried that are consultants now."

- **Tax accountant.** "'Apply the rules.' Which year? 'The year the client
  thinks it is.'"

- **Big Four audit associate, 24.** "'Tick and tie everything.' Then I'd
  bill the hour anyway. That isn't fraud, that's methodology."

- **Management consultant.** "'Find the synergies.' If it refuses, that
  goes in the deck as a finding. Slide twelve. 'No synergies found.' Two
  hundred thousand dollars."

- **Treasury analyst.** "'Tell me where the cash is.' Not the balance.
  Where it *is*. Which bank. Which country. Which Tuesday."

- **Payroll clerk.** "'Pay everyone correctly.' Then I'd stand very still
  and wait for the emails."

- **Executive assistant.** "'Find a time that works for all six of them.'
  It does scheduling? With logic? Does it do egos? Two of them have egos
  and one of them has a boat."

- **Logistics planner.** "'Route the trucks.' If it says 'refused, that is
  a travelling salesman problem,' I'm framing it. Nobody has ever told me
  my problem had a name."

- **Warehouse supervisor.** "'Count what's actually on the shelf.' It
  can't. Nobody can. The spreadsheet's whole job is being wrong in a way
  we've all agreed on."

- **Nonprofit bookkeeper.** "One workbook, eleven tabs, and a grant that
  dies if column F is wrong. Yes. What does it cost? Free? We can't afford
  free either. Someone has to install it."

- **Lab manager.** "'Don't turn the gene names into dates.' If it does
  that one thing, I'll nominate it for something."

- **Investment banking analyst, 2 a.m.** "'Make the model tie.' It asks,
  'tie to what?' Buddy, if I knew that I'd be asleep."

- **Product manager.** "'Show me the metric that goes up.' Any of them.
  I'll build the roadmap around it."

- **Site reliability engineer.** "It has an undo button? Ours is called
  Thursday."

- **Actuary.** "'Estimate when everyone dies.' I already have that
  spreadsheet. I'd just like it to stop being right."

- **Compliance officer.** "It refuses in writing, it never phones home,
  and it logs what ran? Stop. I'm at work."

- **Board member.** "Can you put it on one slide?" *It's a language.* "Can
  you put the language on one slide?"

- **Founder.** "'Extend the runway.' It says that isn't a spreadsheet
  operation. Most honest thing anyone has told me this quarter."

- **Recruiter.** "'Find candidates with ten years in a tool that came out
  last year.' It refuses. So does LinkedIn, but LinkedIn charges."

- **Customer support lead.** "'Close every ticket that's actually fixed.'
  Then 'Reopen the ones that aren't.' Then 'Find out who marked them
  fixed.' It's Kevin. It's always Kevin."

- **Owner of The Master Spreadsheet.** "No. Nobody touches it. Not you,
  not English, not God. There's a tab called DO NOT DELETE and a tab
  called DO NOT DELETE 2, and that's the system."

- **Economist.** "'Assume a spreadsheet.'"

## Third round, as heard at a bank branch after close, on a plant floor, and at one Excel user-group meeting

*Added 2026-09-10, the same evening, on request. Same rules.*

- **Facilities manager.** "'Find the room that's always booked and always
  empty.' It's 4B. I don't need software. I need a lock."

- **Marketing manager.** "'Make the funnel look like a funnel.' It has
  never looked like a funnel. It looks like a bucket with a personal
  problem."

- **Project manager.** "'Move every slipped date to next quarter.' Then,
  'Move next quarter.'"

- **Google Sheets loyalist.** "Does it work in Sheets?" *Not yet.* "So
  you're asking what I'd do with a time machine."

- **Insurance adjuster.** "'Find the claim that's lying.' It found three.
  One of them is mine."

- **Stock trader.** "Last time a spreadsheet broke on my floor it took a
  bank with it. Does this one at least break in English?"

- **Retail store manager.** "'Schedule Saturday.' It does that? With the
  logic? Fine, but Brianna can't work with Tyler, and that's not in the
  system. That's in the parking lot."

- **Quality manager, ISO 9001.** "'Make the SOP match what we actually
  do.' Wait. It *is* the SOP? You can't do that. That's cheating. That's
  exactly what the auditor wants and it's cheating."

- **Plant manager.** "'Schedule the line.' Does it know about Gary?
  Everything depends on Gary. Gary isn't in any table."

- **Union steward.** "'Calculate the overtime correctly.' Finally. Now do
  it in front of management."

- **Bank branch manager, after close.** "'Balance the drawer.' Then,
  'Find the eleven cents.' Then, 'Stop looking for the eleven cents.'"

- **Loan officer.** "'Approve everyone who looks fine.' It refuses. Good.
  Last time we did that was 2007."

- **Accounts payable clerk.** "'Pay the invoice.' Then, 'Pay the invoice
  we already paid, but find out first.' That's two sentences for one
  Tuesday."

- **Cost accountant.** "'Allocate overhead fairly.' It refused. Fair isn't
  a spreadsheet operation, and I've been pretending it was for nine
  years."

- **FP&A director.** "'Explain the variance.' It asked, 'in what order?'
  That's already better than my last analyst."

- **Revenue recognition accountant.** "'Recognize revenue when it's
  earned.' Oh, it wants to know when that is. So does the SEC, pal."

- **Helpdesk technician.** "It can't unblock its own file? Right-click,
  Properties, Unblock. I'm going to say that eleven thousand times."

- **Excel MVP.** "I've seen every add-in. This one says no? The others say
  'sure' and then delete a column."

- **Technical writer.** "It refuses with a full sentence that names the
  problem and the fix? That's my whole job. It's going to take my job
  with a sentence I would have written."

- **Localization manager.** "Same program, rendered in Spanish? I've spent
  fifteen years translating strings that mean nothing. A program that
  means the same thing in both. Don't get me excited."

- **Airline crew scheduler.** "Prolog in a cell. We ran the crews on Prolog
  in 1994. It's in a basement. It still works. Nobody alive can read it."

- **Retired COBOL programmer.** "English-like, business-oriented, readable
  by auditors, deterministic. Kid, I have your manual in my attic. It's
  from 1961."

- **ERP consultant.** "In English? We charge four million to put it in
  German."

- **Grant writer.** "'Make the budget add up to exactly what they'll give
  us.' It refused. The grant officer also refused. I have a type."

## Fourth round, from the far side: as heard in a 2046 break room, a university accounting lab, and one grandparent's kitchen

*Added 2026-09-10, at the owner's question: what would this file sound
like written by people who grew up with Frazaro and never knew the manual
days? The original question does not parse for them — the universal
first answer was "as opposed to what?" — so the interviewer asked instead
what they imagine it was like before. This round breaks the file's rules
on purpose: it cannot be collected, because the people in it do not exist
yet, so it has no replacement rule and nothing in it moves to the retired
section. It is `AUDIT.md` III.5's daydream told from the other side, and
it is the win condition `VIABILITY.md` asked for, said aloud by people who
never saw the war. History confirms it or retires it; nothing else can.*

- **Junior analyst, 22, first job.** "As opposed to what?" *Clicking.*
  "Clicking what?" *Each cell.* "With your hand? Like a wild animal?"

- **Accounting student.** "The exam said 'write a VLOOKUP.' I wrote 'Look
  up the price of the item in the price table.' It worked. I got marked
  down. I filed a refusal."

- **Child at a grandparent's kitchen table, 9.** "Grandpa's workbook has a
  macro in it. We're not allowed to open it. He says it's load-bearing."

- **New controller, has never seen VBA.** "It's a language where the words
  are English and the sentences aren't. Why does it say 'Dim'? Is it
  sad?"

- **Vintage macro collector.** "I collect recorded ones. Select, Select,
  Select, ActiveCell dot FormulaR1C1. It's like listening to someone
  breathe."

- **Museum docent.** "This was a 'ribbon.' And this was the 'Trust
  Center.' No, we don't know what it trusted."

- **Phrasebook lexicographer, Finance department.** "My title is
  lexicographer and my department is Finance, and both of those are
  normal. When my kids were small they thought I was a dictionary."

- **Her colleague.** "Marketing's phrasebook says 'close out' means
  something different from ours. Every quarter there's a war and she
  holds a hearing. Provenance wins. It always wins. Marketing cries."

- **Analyst whose sentence was promoted.** "My phrasing went into the base
  edition last year. 'Roll the forecast forward.' My whole family came."

- **Young auditor.** "I've never read a description of a control. I read
  the control. What did you read?" *A document saying a person followed a
  document.* "That's adorable."

- **Native CFO.** "One page. It says what ran. It has always said what
  ran. Why are you telling me this like it's news?"

- **Native IT administrator.** "Tell me again about the software that
  phoned home. Phoned whom? What did it say?"

- **Old-timer, the only non-native at the table.** "These kids can't write
  a SUMIFS. Ask for one and they say 'add up west region sales' and it
  *works* and it makes me sick."

- **Manager in Osaka.** "Madrid wrote it, I read it in Japanese, London
  audited it, one hash. Why do the old people get emotional when I say
  that?"

- **Teenager.** "Dad makes me write my allowance as a procedure. 'Increase
  allowance by five if room is clean.' It refuses. 'Clean' isn't a
  checked property. Dad says that's the lesson."

- **Historian of computing.** "The era began when one FP&A associate got a
  wrong macro from a robot and got angry. It's not much of a founding
  myth, but it's ours."

- **Nurse, 26.** "The hospital runs on one workbook and everyone knows how
  it works, because it's written down. Cabo is a place we go now. It
  isn't a threat."

- **Pirate Day enthusiast, third generation.** "'Stow the total in cell
  B2, arr.' Forty years. Never once refused. That's what a standard is."

- **Person who has never typed a spreadsheet.** "I talk to it. I've always
  talked to it. What do you mean, 'typed'?"

- **The nostalgic one.** "I kind of wish I'd lived when spreadsheets could
  just be wrong. Sounds relaxing."

- **Payroll clerk, 24.** "They say the old robots would apologize and then
  do it again. Like a dog. A dog with your payroll."

- **Office that does Manual Fridays.** "Once a month we click everything by
  hand. It's for morale. Someone always cries."

- **Child asking about Kevin.** "Was Kevin real?" *Kevin was what they
  called the log before the log existed.* "So Kevin was a log." *Kevin was
  a person.* "That's worse."

- **Gary's grandson, plant floor.** "Grandpa was never in a table.
  Everything depended on him. Now he's a row. He's fine with it. He says
  it's quieter."

## The three that are field notes wearing a joke

Kevin is the change log — the question of who changed what, which no
engine on the shelf yet answers and Contemplation 5 files under
reflection. Cabo is the bus factor — the one person who knows how the
spreadsheet works, which is the fragility `VENTURE.md` §2 sells against.
The senior accountant is the incentive problem from Contemplation 6 —
the person asked to write the procedure down is the person it makes
replaceable — said out loud by the only person who would say it.

The rest are jokes, and a joke that lands with a stranger is also data:
it says which premise they already hold. "I want the one that says no" is
the refusal doctrine, heard from the outside. "Whose English?" is
`ADVOCATUS.md` A.2 in five words. "So it's COBOL" is the objection the
Lisp audience will make first, and the answer is in `README.md`.

The second round carries four more. The compliance officer is the three
differentiators — refusal in writing, no network, a run log — heard from
the outside in one breath, which is `SIG.2`'s one-pager written by its
reader; the third of the three is not built yet, and the joke assumes it
is. The nonprofit bookkeeper is the install cliff: "we can't afford free
either" is the sententia about the first ninety seconds, said by the
person who falls. The warehouse supervisor is Contemplation 4's warning
that determinism is not correctness — a wrong number everyone has agreed
on is still a wrong number. And the owner of The Master Spreadsheet is
change control in its natural habitat: two tabs named DO NOT DELETE is a
version-control system, and it is the one Frazaro has to be better than.

The third round carries five. The helpdesk technician is the first
instruction in the README's install section, said by the person who will
repeat it; the Unblock step is the cliff `MARKETING.md` §1 puts above the
download link. The FP&A director is Contemplation 5's attribution engine,
and "in what order" is its one hard design question, asked by the buyer.
The quality manager is `AUDIT.md` III.1 — the SOP stops being a document
about the procedure and becomes the procedure — and "that's cheating" is
the reaction `SIG.6` exists to collect from a real auditor. The
localization manager is Contemplation 2, programs stored as forms and
rendered in the reader's language, wanted by the person whose job it
would change. And the retired COBOL programmer is `ADVOCATUS.md` A.1's
graveyard speaking for itself, with that document's own how-wrong
counter attached: COBOL won its era on exactly this pitch, and a manual
in an attic is a survivor, not a headstone.

The fourth round is field notes end to end, because a native's
bafflement is a doctrine stated backwards. The pirate's forty years is
`SD-4`, a shipped spelling keeps its meaning, heard as a family
tradition. The manager in Osaka is Contemplation 4's cross-lingual
institution — one hash, three languages — as an ordinary Tuesday. The
lexicographer and her colleague are Contemplation 1's register problem
and Contemplation 4's new profession, arrived and unremarkable, with
provenance settling the quarterly war exactly as `GO.1` says it should.
The student marked down for a working sentence is `AUDIT.md` III.3,
teaching without the syntax tax, colliding with an exam that has not
heard. The young auditor is `AUDIT.md` III.1 and the compliance pack: the
control is the thing read, and the old attestation is "adorable." Kevin
becoming the log is Contemplation 5's reflection engine, which is the
change log nobody has today. The docent's Trust Center is
`SUBSTRATE.md`'s quarantine history, curated. And the old-timer who feels
sick is `ADVOCATUS.md` A.5 answered the only way it could be: the clerk
never had to become a programmer, and that is what the premise meant.
