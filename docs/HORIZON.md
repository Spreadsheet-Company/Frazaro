# HORIZON - the spreadsheet of 2096

*`SINGULARITY.md` draws a line and lays the road to it: the Analyst ex Machina, a spreadsheet that answers a manager as an analyst would, deterministically, with a proof attached. The owner's question on 2026-09-30, the day after that road was filed, was what lies past the line. Church was born before the first computer and lived to see the Internet, and he could not have written the computer of 1995 in the notation of 1925. In like manner: what does a spreadsheet look like in 2096, once the Singularity is a floor rather than a summit, and could a 2026 mind fathom it? This file is the answer given that day, from a council the owner convened (Peirce, Church, Turing, McCarthy and Graham, with Quine and Codd called in) and three novelists (Wells, Herbert, Orwell). It is science fiction on purpose. Its job is to test the boundary between science and fiction and to say, for every idea, which side it falls on.*

*What this document is not. It is not a commitment, nothing in it is an item, and nothing in it carries a handle; `SINGULARITY.md`'s stages are the road, and this is the horizon the road points at. An idea here that is ever picked up goes through `SINGULARITY.md` if it is third-gear work, or straight onto `BETA_ROADMAP.md` under the department that owns it, and takes an ID there. Every standing decision in the register stands, and the two that are load-bearing in `SINGULARITY.md` are load-bearing here: `SD-13` (no outbound network call, ever) and the owner's veto of `GENSYM` ("determinism is the Chinese wall between this project and the Wild West of LLMs"). The spreadsheet of 2096 described below is deterministic all the way down. Where an idea seems to strain that, the strain is named.*

*House rules, as for every ledger in `docs/`: append-only; a correction is added and dated, never written over; where this file and the code disagree, the code is right; where this file and `SINGULARITY.md` disagree, `SINGULARITY.md` is right, since it is nearer. The figure rule takes a local form here. Every idea is marked **math exists** (the result is published and the only work is building it in), **engineering** (nothing new has to be true, but someone has to build it) or **fiction** (it needs the world to change, usually socially). An idea with no mark is a vibe, and the file does not keep vibes.*

## 1. The cut, and whether a 2026 mind can see past it

A Dedekind cut is the right figure, and it answers the question about the mind before anything else. √2 has no finite expansion in the digits of the rationals, yet a rational mind can name it, bound it on both sides, and compute it to any precision it needs. `SINGULARITY.md` is the lower set; its seven stages are the Cauchy sequence. What a 2026 mind cannot do is write the limit in 2026 notation. So this file is a guess at the next digits: each idea one step past the last, its lineage named, its status marked. The owner's instruction was to go further beyond. Charles V's version was *plus ultra*, the *non* struck off the Pillars of Hercules, and there is a phrasebook for it in `scripts/polyglotta/`.

What Church could not see in 1925 was never speed. It was four things: that his calculus would be the *language* of the machines, not a foundation for arithmetic; that the machine would be personal; that it would become a medium between people; and that programs would be data to other programs. Each has a spreadsheet twin, and the twins are the skeleton of what follows: the grammar as the language of the institutional record (section 2); the personal record (section 5, item 12); the proof as the thing exchanged between parties (section 5, items 2 and 3); and sentences as data to other sentences (the casebook of section 4, the macros the first gear already has).

## 2. The four leaps that make 2026 the abacus

The abacus was arithmetic. VisiCalc's leap in 1979 was *dependency*: a cell is a named expression and the sheet is one enormous `letrec` (Excel is the most-used functional language on Earth, as Peyton Jones has argued since 2003). Past the cut, the leaps are:

| From | To | Whose |
|---|---|---|
| a value | a sign: value, object, interpretant. Every number carries what it measures and how it was derived | Peirce |
| a number | a proof: a number without a derivation is a *different type*, rendered as such, as unsigned code is | Necula, 1997 |
| a state | a history: the record is the object, the grid is a view of it | Orwell, negated |
| a dependency | an obligation: `should`, `must`, `never`, over records | Austin, von Wright |

The abacus moved beads. The spreadsheet moved numbers. The thing of 2096 moves *norms*. And the word "spreadsheet" goes the way of "computer": it named a grid; it will name a role. The job title is already waiting. A controller is one who writes controls, and in 2096 that is literal.

## 3. The council, each with one bequest

**Church.** The Church-Turing thesis says every effective procedure is a lambda. The Church-Frazaro thesis, 2096: every answer an analyst gives from a workbook alone is a sentence of a finite grammar over the workbook's relations. By then it has a theorem's shape. The repertoire of a close is characterized as a language class, and a grammar's *completeness* over it is proven against the Imitation corpus (`SINGULARITY.md`, Stage 6.4) rather than hoped. The wild version: the grammar's growth has a rate, measured in sentences per refusal, and in 2096 the rate has flattened, not because thought stopped but because the close is finite, as `SINGULARITY.md` section 1 bets. *Engineering.*

**Turing.** The universal machine. In 2096 the question "is this a spreadsheet, a database, a program, a contract or a statute?" has no answer, because Turing abolished the distinction in 1936 and finance was the last field to notice. And the Imitation Game runs in reverse: junior analysts train *against the machine*. The test is whether the human can match its proof discipline, and the education is ten thousand refusals in words. `SINGULARITY.md` section 2 already lists teaching as one of the eight duties ("a refusal with directions is teaching"); this is that duty grown up. *Fiction, social.*

**McCarthy.** Three bequests. The Advice Taker (1958): a program that accepts declarative sentences at runtime and reasons with them without being reprogrammed. That is the Definitions sheet (Stage 1.5), sixty-eight years early. Elaboration tolerance (1998): a representation is good when adding a fact costs no rewrite. In 2096 it is the product metric: how many sentences did the new control cost? The gap ledger (Stage 6.1) measures it. And `eval`: the workbook carries its own interpreter. Call it the Rosetta workbook: a file holding its sentences, its phrasebook and its proofs, so that opened in 2096 on a machine with no vendor left alive, it reads itself to its reader. `SD-4` is the promise that makes it possible, since a sentence written in 2026 keeps its meaning. A workbook as a letter to the future. *Buildable this year, and the fun one; section 5, item 14.*

**Peirce.** He already owns `SINGULARITY.md` section 8. The bequest not yet collected is the existential graphs. Peirce drew his logic on a surface he named the *sheet of assertion*: writing a proposition on the sheet asserts it; an oval cut around it negates it; a line between two spots identifies them. Alpha graphs are propositional, Beta first-order, Gamma modal. The Controls sheet of Stage 1 is the sheet of assertion with the name filed off: writing `No cell in column G of sheet Invoices should contain "Unpaid".` on it asserts it, and topic lines (Stage 0.5) are already spatial scope. The 2096 move is that *placement is logic*: a boxed region of the sheet is a cut, the sentences inside it are negated or hypothetical (`Under the scenario "Rebate in September":` is a Gamma cut), and the manager reasons by moving things on the surface. The spreadsheet is the only mass-market two-dimensional medium humans have; Peirce designed a logic for exactly that and died in 1914 without a grid to draw it on. That he named it the sheet of assertion is the kind of clue this project treats as evidence. *Math exists; the surface is fiction.*

Peirce again: inquiry begins in the irritation of doubt (1877). The gap ledger is a doubt ledger. In 2096 the grammar has been grown for seventy years from what people could not say: a language whose etymology is refusals.

**Quine.** "To be is to be the value of a bound variable": an org's ontology is exactly what its Definitions sheet quantifies over, and nothing else exists to the machine. Then the maxim of minimum mutilation (1970): when an observation contradicts the web of belief, revise as little as possible. That is `ABDUCE`'s parsimony ranking (`SINGULARITY.md` section 8.2), stated by a philosopher fifty-six years early. And the indeterminacy of translation, *gavagai*: Polyglotta claims `english.vla` and `espanol.vla` compile to the same form. In 2096 that claim is enforced by goldens across forty phrasebooks, and a Tokyo controller's controls run unchanged in São Paulo. Translation determinacy as a floor that never goes down. *Engineering.*

**Graham.** "The Hundred-Year Language" (2003) asks the owner's question for programming languages and answers it: the survivors have the smallest axiomatic core and waste the most cycles for convenience. The hundred-year spreadsheet is section 7. The Blub paradox answers the other half of the question: a Blub programmer cannot see macros, because from Blub they look like unnecessary weirdness. So the features of 2096 are findable today. They are whatever looks like unnecessary weirdness in Frazaro from inside Excel: proofs for everything, refusal in words, a tokenizer that will not guess, a record that cannot be edited, a wall against the most powerful technology of the decade. Those are not eccentricities. They are the parts of 2096 that arrived early.

## 4. The three novelists

**Wells** pre-wrote this project twice. *World Brain* (1938) proposed a permanent, constantly revised encyclopedia of everything known. The 2096 version is a World Brain of *verified figures*: not facts asserted but numbers with derivations, exchanged as proofs. And in *The Shape of Things to Come* (1933) the world language of 2106 is Basic English, Ogden's 850 words, a finite grammar for a planet. Wells was wrong about the planet and right about the close. *Fiction, and he got there first.*

**Herbert.** The Butlerian Jihad: "Thou shalt not make a machine in the likeness of a human mind." The `GENSYM` veto is a one-line Jihad and the most *Dune* thing in the repository. Mentats: humans who compute and are trusted *because* their method can be stated. Other Memory: the Bene Gesserit carry their ancestors' memories, and the explanation phrasebook (`SINGULARITY.md` section 8.3) is Other Memory for a company. The analyst who explained the rebate timing in 2031 retired in 2050, and her schema fires in 2096. Prescience: Paul sees the futures that follow from the present. `WHATIF` over a scenario tree (Stage 5.3) is deterministic prescience: it sees only the futures that follow from stated hypotheses, which is why it is trustworthy and why it is not Paul. The Golden Path: `OPTIMIZE` over time, the narrowest path that keeps every `must` green for forty quarters. *The Jihad is policy; the casebook is Stage 4.2; the Golden Path is engineering, large.*

**Orwell.** "Who controls the past controls the future: who controls the present controls the past." The 2096 record has no memory hole: the past is the object and the present is derived from it, so controlling the present controls nothing. `2 + 2 = 5` is refused in words, with the row number. Newspeak shrank the dictionary to shrink thought; this grammar is its inverse. It only grows, by sentences people needed (`SD-7`), and `SD-4` means a word never changes its meaning under the person who wrote it. Orwell himself began sympathetic to Basic English and wrote Newspeak as the fear of what a controlled language becomes in the wrong hands. The difference is who holds the dictionary: Minitrue, or the gap ledger. *Policy.*

## 5. The next digits

1. **The audited plug.** The eternal sin is a constant typed over a formula. In 2096 it is a speech act with a deadline: `Plug cell D17 of sheet Model as 4,500 until the October close because the vendor file is late.` Green until October, red after, and `Sign off` refuses while an expired plug stands. *Engineering; a Stage 1 sentence, buildable now.*

2. **Proof-carrying commerce.** An invoice arrives as a table with the derivation of its total, and the receiver's controls run on the derivation, not the number. Business-to-business is proof exchange; XBRL is remembered as the attempt that shipped the number without the proof. By file, never by socket: `SD-13` is why anyone trusts it. *Engineering.*

3. **Zero-knowledge reconciliation.** `Every row of our Bank should match a row of their Ledger.` proven without either side showing its ledger (vSQL, 2017, verifiable SQL over data the verifier does not hold, is the seed). An audit in 2096 is a proof exchange, not a site visit. *Math exists; the strain on the wall is nil, since the proof travels as a file the user carries.*

4. **The dialogue-game close.** Lorenzen, 1950s: a proposition is true when the proponent has a winning strategy against every challenge. The 2096 workbook is the proponent. The auditor types `I dispute cell B9 of sheet Summary.`; the model answers with the derivation or concedes a plug; `This close is final.` means the proponent won every challenge, and the record keeps the game. *Math exists.*

5. **Dimensional typing.** Every number has a dimension: a currency as of a date, a period, a count, thousands. `You cannot add September to a dollar.` is a Check refusal. The Mars Climate Orbiter never happens in a spreadsheet again. *Engineering; `UNITS` is already in `SINGULARITY.md`'s engines table.*

6. **Deterministic surprise.** No statistics, but the record gives envelopes: `Cell B9 is outside the range of every prior close.` is exact arithmetic over the snapshot series (Stage 3.4). The 2096 workbook raises its hand with Peirce's surprising fact, and the catalogue answers. *Engineering.*

7. **Incremental everything.** Differential dataflow (2013) and DBSP (2022): a fixpoint is never recomputed, only updated. So provenance is *maintained* rather than reconstructed, `why` is instant, and every what-if ever asked stays live and re-answers on every edit: the always-on possibility space. `check_datalog_per_tuple_alloc` is the ghost of this constraint, and `VLA_Browser.bas`, the host-free translate API for ports, is the WebAssembly digit already in the file list. *Math exists.*

8. **The literate close.** Knuth, 1984. The annual report *is* the model: each sentence of the management discussion is a sentence of the grammar, each figure is a cell, and `EnglishRenderForm` renders the model as prose. The report, the control and the tutorial are one object three ways. The 2096 CFO reads the model aloud to the board, because it fits on paper. *Engineering.*

9. **A company is its dictionary.** Due diligence is reading the Definitions sheet. An acquisition merges two dictionaries, conflicts refused by name. The analyst's job after the Singularity is lexicographer. *Fiction, social.*

10. **The grammar leaves finance.** `No two voices should move in parallel fifths.` is a Controls sheet over a score (Fux, 1725). Zoning law, chess, a clinical protocol, a constitution: every rule-system humans have written has the shape `No X should Y` over a record. The abacus was for numbers; the thing of 2096 is for norms, and finance was merely where the record was best kept. *The engines exist and take any table; the phrasebooks are fiction.*

11. **The verified kernel.** CompCert, seL4. In 2096 the trusted base of finance is a few thousand proven lines and everything else is sentences over it; the auditor runs the checks on the checker. The METAPROOF line is the lineage. *Science exists; engineering enormous.*

12. **The personal constitution.** Church could not see the personal computer. `No month should end with savings below 2,000.` over a household's own bank table; `Why did the grocery total rise?` answered from the catalogue. The Analyst ex Machina for a household: Fremen water discipline as a Controls sheet. *Fiction, social; the engines are identical.*

13. **The grid as TTY.** The grid never dies. In 2096 it is what the terminal is today: the power user's surface, loved, retro, and still where the sentences are typed. Column B and column C are forever. *An observation, kept because it is the one line here a 2026 mind can be sure of.*

14. **The Rosetta workbook** (McCarthy's, section 3). A workbook that holds its sentences, its phrasebook and its proofs, with a sheet that says *open me in 2096*. Written this year, it is the cheapest experiment in this file, and the only one whose result arrives on schedule. *Engineering, small; a candidate for `SPITBALLS.md`'s next sitting.*

## 6. The axis a 2026 mind cannot see, and the big guess

Church's blind spot was never speed; it was *who* and *what for*. So the honest guess at the unfathomable axis is social, and it is this: in 2096 the heaviest users of deterministic spreadsheets are machines. The statistical minds draft; the records judge. "The model proposes; Frazaro disposes" (`SINGULARITY.md` section 1, `LE.7`) scales from one boundary in one tool to the architecture of trust of a civilization that learned, expensively, that plausible text is not a record. No number enters a filing without a derivation an auditor can replay. The decade of the large language model is remembered as the Age of Plausibility, and what ended it was not a better model but a *worse* one: a machine that can only say what it can prove, and refuses the rest in words. Orwell's Ministry, defeated by Peirce's sheet of assertion. *Fiction, and the kind this project is already building.*

## 7. The hundred-year spreadsheet, in six axioms

Graham's test: the smallest core wins.

1. A cell is a named pure expression. (Church)
2. A table is a relation. (Codd)
3. A sentence is a checked act: a question, a control, a command, a definition, a declaration. (Austin)
4. A value carries its derivation; one that does not is a different type. (Peirce, Necula)
5. The record is append-only; the grid is a view of it. (Orwell, negated)
6. Nothing is inferred statistically. (The wall. The Jihad.)

Everything else is a phrasebook. Frazaro at `0.7.1` has four of the six: the cell is Excel's, the table is `VLA_Relation`'s, the checked act is the first gear, and the sixth is the veto. The road to the other two is written down: the record is `AXM.4`, filed; provenance is `SINGULARITY.md`'s Stage 2.4, still a handle.

## 9. The upper set: what the spreadsheet of 2096 does that the Analyst ex Machina cannot

The lower set first, so the cut is sharp. The Analyst ex Machina, fully built, does eight things over one workbook: it prepares, checks, answers, explains, remembers, rehearses, teaches and signs. It reads the workbook as relations, runs controls written in words, answers questions with proofs, says what changed and why from a finite catalogue, rehearses one hypothesis at a time on a copy, keeps a record, refuses everything else by name, and never guesses. Everything below is something that machine *cannot* do, each with the line in `SINGULARITY.md` where it says so, the sentence a manager would type, the two replies side by side, the mechanism that makes the 2096 reply a computation someone can replay, and the mark. The wall holds in every row: the 2096 machine never guesses either, which is why the list can be finite and specific.

### 9.1 Proof: from this case to every case

**1. For all inputs.** The hypothesis engines test one point: `WHATIF` is "copy, set, recalculate, read, delete" (Stage 5.1); `GOAL` finds "one value, or no such value" (5.2); and the formula reader parses references, "not yet the whole formula language" (0.2).

- Sentence: `Can cell D9 of sheet Model ever be negative?`
- Analyst ex Machina: `I can test one value of an input; name it.`
- 2096: `No. For every input within the ranges on sheet Assumptions, D9 is at least 0; it reaches 0 when B2 is 0.` Or: `Yes: when B2 is 0 and C3 is 401, D9 is -12.` Also `Which inputs can ever change the sign of D9?` and `Which hiring plan keeps every must green for forty quarters at the least cost?` (the feasible region over time, not one scenario at a time).
- Mechanism: the formula language as data, then symbolic reasoning over it; an SMT solver is deterministic under a fixed strategy and times out into a refusal in words, never into a guess. The Analyst ex Machina answers *this* case; 2096 answers *every* case. *Math exists.*

**2. Equivalence, and refactoring with a proof.** `DIFF` says what changed (3.1); nothing says whether it matters.

- Sentence: `Does sheet Model compute the same D9 as "Model 2095.xlsx" for every input?`
- Analyst ex Machina: `Three formulas differ: D4, D7 and D9. Here they are.`
- 2096: `Yes, for every input.` Or: `No: they differ when C3 exceeds 400. One input where they differ: C3 = 401; the old model gives 1,240, this one gives 1,252.` Also `Replace the 40 formulas in column D with one formula, proven equal.`
- Mechanism: symbolic equivalence over two formula graphs. The one machine shows a difference; the other proves an absence of one. *Math exists.*

**3. The controls as a theory, and the machine's own ignorance as a list.** The Analyst ex Machina runs controls against data, never against each other, and reflects the workbook, not itself.

- Sentences: `Can every control on sheet Controls be green at once?` `Which controls are redundant?` `Which control could never fire?` `What can you not explain about sheet Summary?`
- Analyst ex Machina: runs them and reports colours.
- 2096: `Controls 3 and 14 can never both be green: 3 requires every invoice paid within 30 days, 14 defines overdue at 45 days, and an invoice paid on day 40 violates 3 while satisfying 14. One of them is wrong.` `Control 9 is implied by control 2 and the definition of overdue; it can be deleted without loss.` `Control 21 has been green for 120 closes and could not have been otherwise: its range is empty.` `Nine inputs carry no provenance; two schemas have never fired; control 14 has never been tested against a planted failure.`
- Mechanism: satisfiability and entailment over the controls as rules; answer-set programming, and clingo is already the project's oracle. *Math exists.*

**4. The sheet checks arguments, not only answers.** `SD-16` forbids anaphora, and the Q&A sheet is a list of independent acts; there is no `therefore`.

- Sentences, as rows 8 to 11 of a Q&A sheet: `B9 rose by 6,300.` `C5 rose by 6,300 and feeds B9.` `No other input of B9 changed.` `Therefore the change in B9 is explained by C5.`
- Analyst ex Machina: answers rows 8 to 10 separately and refuses row 11.
- 2096: row 11 is green if it follows from rows 8 to 10 under the rules, red with the missing step named if it does not (`Row 10 does not establish that C5 feeds B9 at coefficient 1; see cell D4.`). The Q&A sheet becomes a proof script, and a dispute is a game played on it (item 18).
- Mechanism: proof checking over sentences already in the grammar. *Math exists.*

**5. Trusted by proof, not by test.** The Analyst ex Machina is held by floors, goldens and static checks (`SINGULARITY.md` section 3), and by the owner's word.

- Sentence: `Prove that Check accepts no sentence Run would misread.`
- Analyst ex Machina: `There are 1,564 tests and 30 checks; here are their results.` (the counts `SINGULARITY.md` section 3 gives at `0.7.1`)
- 2096: `Here is the proof, and here is the 200-line verifier that checks it; read the verifier.` The file carries the certificate, and the vendor leaves the trust base.
- Mechanism: a verified kernel, the CompCert and seL4 lineage; METAPROOF is its first step. *Math exists; engineering enormous.*

### 9.2 Memory: from one workbook to everything that ever happened

**6. The whole estate.** The Analyst ex Machina is "anchored at a cell, budgeted, and interruptible" (section 9) and reads one workbook plus one other file read-only (0.3).

- Sentences: `Which workbooks in Finance still use the 2095 tax rate?` `Which of our 4,000 models reference a sheet that no longer exists?` `Which model does the board pack's cell C12 ultimately come from?`
- Analyst ex Machina: `I read one workbook. Open the one you mean.`
- 2096: `Fourteen. Here they are, with the cell in each.` A number in the board pack has a cone that runs through six workbooks and ends in a bank file, and `why` walks all of it.
- Mechanism: `REFLECT` over the estate, indexed and maintained incrementally, so no question is refused for size. *Engineering.*

**7. The complete past.** One Undo snapshot per run, a snapshot per close, a run log (section 3; 3.4): the Analyst ex Machina can say what changed since a snapshot, not who did it or when within the interval.

- Sentences: `Who typed the 4,500 in cell D17 of sheet Model, when, and what did the controls say at that moment?` `Had control 14 existed in 2091, when would it first have fired?` `Show me every value cell B9 has ever held, with the act that set each one.`
- Analyst ex Machina: `D17 differs from the August snapshot.` It cannot answer the rest.
- 2096: `Signed by key 7F3A at 2096-03-02 16:41; controls 2 and 14 were green, 9 was yellow. The 4,500 was typed over a formula that read =SUM(D3:D16).` `Control 14 would first have fired at the 2091-11 close, on rows 41 and 42.`
- Mechanism: every act in an append-only record; the present is a fold of the past; a new control replays against the whole history. *Engineering.*

**8. Why that bottoms out in the world.** `cause` stops at `input(X)`, "a cell with no formula" (3.2), and "a control over a Table whose rows are wrong passes green" (section 9).

- Sentence: `Why is cell B4 of sheet Invoices 12,400?`
- Analyst ex Machina: `B4 is an input: it has no formula.`
- 2096: `B4 is the total of invoice 1042 from Acme, signed by Acme's record on 2096-03-04: 40 units at 310. Bank statement line 8812, signed by the bank, corroborates the payment on 2096-03-19.` And `Which inputs of sheet Summary carry no provenance from outside this workbook?` lists the nine that were typed by a person.
- Mechanism: numbers arrive as proof-carrying tables by file, signed at the source, and the receiver's `why` continues into the sender's derivation. No socket; `SD-13` holds. What stays wrong is the world itself, which no set contains. *Engineering; the adoption is fiction.*

**9. Always on.** `GOAL` runs when asked, on a copy, and discards (5.2); `WHATIF` likewise.

- Sentence: `Keep answering: what would cell B2 have to be for cell D9 to be 0?`
- Analyst ex Machina: answers once.
- 2096: the answer sits in column C and changes as the model does, with every what-if the manager ever asked beside it, each live; the possibility space is a view of the model, not a rehearsal of it.
- Mechanism: incremental maintenance of fixpoints and derivations (differential dataflow, 2013; DBSP, 2022): nothing is recomputed, only updated, so a standing question costs nothing to keep. *Math exists.*

### 9.3 Invention, deterministically: the machine proposes, in words, with its evidence, and a person ratifies

**10. Induced definitions.** Definitions are written by the person who owns the word (1.5, 6.2); the Analyst ex Machina never writes one.

- Sentence: `Define stale from the rows I marked.`
- Analyst ex Machina: `A definition is a sentence. Write it: An X is stale when ...`
- 2096: `The simplest rule that covers all 23 rows you marked and none of the 410 you did not: A row of Table Invoices is stale when its Date is more than 90 days before today and its Status is not "Closed". Two rows fit no rule of four clauses or fewer: rows 61 and 88. Look at them. Adopt the definition?`
- Mechanism: enumeration of the definition grammar in a fixed order, smallest first, scored by exact coverage, ties by catalogue order. Flash Fill for rules, with the work shown; Excel has had deterministic synthesis from examples since 2013, for strings. The proof of fit is part of the answer. *Math exists.*

**11. Induced schemas.** "Abduction cannot invent a schema" (8.3); the honest answer is `No explanation in the catalogue accounts for the change in B9.`

- Sentence: `Why did cell B9 change?` when nothing in the catalogue fits.
- Analyst ex Machina: the honest refusal, and the inputs that changed.
- 2096: `No schema in the catalogue accounts for B9. The smallest schema in the schema grammar that would: a row of Table Sales is booked in the month after its Date. It accounts for the whole 6,300 and predicts that F12 is 6,300 above August, which holds. Add it to the catalogue as "late booking"?`
- Mechanism: the schema language of 8.3 (signature, prediction, test) is finite; search it in order; keep the smallest schema whose prediction passes. The person names it and adopts it, so institutional knowledge still enters through a sentence, but the machine drafts the sentence from the evidence. *Engineering.*

**12. Induced controls, from the record of what went wrong.**

- Sentence: `Which controls would have caught the anomalies of the last twelve closes before sign-off?`
- Analyst ex Machina: nothing of the kind.
- 2096: `Of 31 anomalies explained in the record, 19 were plugs. One control would have caught 17 of them: No formula column of sheet Model should contain a typed constant. Two more would catch 6 of the remaining 12. Adopt?`
- Mechanism: a search over the controls grammar for the control with the best exact coverage of the recorded anomalies, ties in a fixed order. Learning in the sense that matters, with no statistics anywhere. *Engineering.*

**13. Induced grammar.** `SD-7` schedules a grammar section when a sentence needs it, and `LE.7` lets a model draft sentences in the *published* grammar. Neither proposes grammar.

- Sentence: `What are people trying to say that you refuse?`
- Analyst ex Machina: the gap ledger (6.1), refused sentences clustered by the rule they nearly matched.
- 2096: `212 of 300 refused sentences this quarter share one shape: Which <rows of a Table> have no <related rows>? Here is the rule that accepts them, the form it compiles to, the proof corpus it would need, and a proof that no shipped sentence changes meaning if it is added.` The last clause is `SD-4` as a theorem, checked mechanically against the goldens.
- Mechanism: grammatical inference over a finite space of rule shapes. The language grows from its refusals, and a human still ratifies every rule. *Engineering.*

**14. It asks.** The Analyst ex Machina refuses by naming what it needs (section 1), once per question; it never conducts an interview.

- Sentence: `What do you need from me?`
- Analyst ex Machina: `To answer "which invoices are stale" I need a definition of stale.`
- 2096: `Three schemas remain for B9. One question decides between the first and the other two: did the September vendor file arrive? If yes, it is the late booking. If no, I need one more thing: is cell C5 in thousands?`
- Mechanism: a decision tree over the surviving candidates, the question chosen as the one that splits them most evenly, ties in catalogue order. The direction of the dialogue reverses: the detective asks and the manager answers, and every step is still a sentence on the sheet. *Math exists.*

### 9.4 Action: from answering to doing, under a policy a person wrote

**15. Prevention.** "Controls detect; they do not prevent" (section 9); a `must` is checked at Run.

- Sentence: `Cell B9 of sheet Summary must equal cell C4 of sheet GL.`, and then someone types 4,500 into D17.
- Analyst ex Machina: at the next Run, row 8 goes red.
- 2096: the cell refuses the keystroke: `4,500 in D17 would make control 8 fail (B9 would be 6,300 above GL's C4). Refused.` A paste of 400 rows lands whole with every `must` green or does not land at all. `Column F of sheet Sales holds dollars.` makes a pasted date in F a refusal at entry, not a finding at audit. A typed-over constant stops being a thing one discovers and becomes a thing one cannot do without a sentence (`Plug cell D17 as 4,500 until the October close because the vendor file is late.`).
- Mechanism: the control engine sits before the edit instead of after it; transactional sheets. *Engineering; the strain is the host, which has to be built with the engine in that seat.*

**16. Standing orders.** The Analyst ex Machina runs at open or at Run (section 4); its grammar tables (section 6) have no `whenever`, no `every weekday`, no `when ... then`.

- Mechanism: time and events as named inputs of the record; the clock is an input the record states, which is `WHATIF`'s own rule (5.1) turned into a feature, since a rehearsal of a standing order runs under a stated time and never the real one. *Engineering.*

**17. Signed by.** "The record says signed as, never signed by, until the host offers something stronger" (section 9).

- Sentence: `Who signed off on sheet Summary, and can they deny it?`

- 2096: `Signed by the holder of key 7F3A at 09:04 against hash 3e1f..., countersigned by the auditor's key 22C0 at 09:30. No.`
- Mechanism: deterministic digital signatures over the record's hash (Ed25519 is deterministic; the SHA-256 is already in `VLA_Digest.bas` and held to vectors by `check_hash_twin`). Verification is local, keys travel as the person chooses, and `SD-13` holds. *Math exists.*

**18. Many parties.** One user and one workbook; a reconciliation needs both tables in front of it (1.4).

- Sentence: `Every row of our Table Bank should match a row of Acme's Ledger on Amount and Date.`
- Analyst ex Machina: `I need Acme's Ledger as a Table.`
- 2096: Acme sends a proof, not the ledger. The control turns green, or names the unmatched rows on our side, and neither party saw the other's book. `I dispute cell B9.`, typed from the auditor's seat, opens a game the record keeps: the model answers with the derivation or concedes a plug, and `This close is final.` means the proponent answered every challenge. Two companies sharing one record, each with its own controls over it: `This contract is executed.` is green when both sides' musts are.
- Mechanism: verifiable queries over data the verifier does not hold (vSQL, 2017) for the first; Lorenzen's dialogue games for the second; a shared append-only record for the third. By file, never by socket. *Math exists; the shared contract is fiction.*

**19. Actuation.** The Analyst ex Machina's answers go in cells.

- Sentence: `No pallet should leave Dock 3 unweighed.`
- Analyst ex Machina: a control over `Table Weighings`, run at open; yesterday's pallets, in column C.
- 2096: the weighbridge is an input device, the dock light is an output device, the sentence is unchanged, and it fires in the world as the pallet moves.
- Mechanism: deterministic, trivially. The strain is `SD-13`'s spirit, since the machine reaches outside itself. *Fiction, with a wall question that is the owner's.*

## 10. The host: tenant, competitor, or compiler

*The owner's question, the same sitting: why should Frazaro be an Excel add-in rather than a standalone WebAssembly spreadsheet that replaces Excel? Wind the clock back to 1986: Microsoft chooses VBA, Frazaro chooses VLA. Why settle for a landlord whose attention is on language models rather than on the frontier of spreadsheets? And, mid-answer, the owner's own reframe: Frazaro as "a spreadsheet generator", a spreadsheet that makes spreadsheets, for Excel, Sheets, Numbers, LibreOffice, or any other target file type. `SUBSTRATE.md` had already read the landlord's thirty-three-year record; this section does not re-derive it, and cites it.*

### 10.1 The short answer

Neither tenant nor competitor: **compiler.** A compiler has targets, not landlords. GCC does not rent from Intel, LaTeX does not compete with Acrobat, and Pandoc never asked Word's permission. Frazaro's lease is not VBA; it is `.xlsx`, which is ISO/IEC 29500 (*exhibit*, 2008), which the landlord wrote and cannot stop opening without ceasing to be Excel. The add-in is the first target because the close is in that building; the second target is the formula layer, which every host has and no macro gate touches; the third is JavaScript. The owner's reframe is the answer to the owner's question, and the shelf had already said it three times without using the word (10.5).

### 10.2 1986, done properly

One date corrected, because it changes the lesson: Microsoft chose VBA in 1993 (Excel 5), after the spreadsheet war was already won (*exhibit*). 1986 is a different anniversary. AutoLISP shipped in AutoCAD 2.18 in January 1986 (*exhibit*): a Lisp inside a commodity product, and the reason AutoCAD became unassailable, since the ecosystem of routines lived in the users' drawings and not in Autodesk's building. The hypothetical year is the year the Lisp-inside-the-product strategy was proven. The asymmetry the owner feels is that Autodesk owned both halves and Frazaro owns one.

The graves on the other road are specific (*exhibits*):

- **Javelin, 1985.** A model-first system where time was a first-class axis (Stage 3.4, forty years early), formulas lived apart from views, and every number had a lineage. It beat Excel 1.0 for InfoWorld's Software Product of the Year. Sold off by 1988.
- **Lotus Improv, 1991.** The better grid: data, views and formulas separated, named dimensions instead of cell addresses. Withdrawn by the mid-1990s; Quantrix sells its descendant to a niche today.

Both were *better models of a spreadsheet*, and both lost to the grid's "worse". Gabriel wrote the theory of it the same year Improv shipped ("The Rise of Worse is Better", 1991): the right thing loses to the thing that spreads, and the way the right thing survives is inside the thing that spreads. Graham's own answer to his own essay was Viaweb: Lisp *inside* the commodity platform, the language as the secret weapon, never the platform as the product. "Beating the Averages" is an argument for the add-in.

How Excel actually beat 1-2-3 is the lesson, and it was not the VBA choice. Excel won by riding a platform shift the incumbent missed (Windows; Lotus bet on OS/2 and shipped a poor 1-2-3 for Windows in 1991), by reading `.wk1` files and offering 1-2-3 keystroke help (compatibility with the incumbent's *files*), and by the incumbent's complacency (*exhibits*). Translated: be where the users are going, and read their files perfectly. The platform shift happening now is the one the landlord is riding, the probabilistic one, and Frazaro's bet is the counter-bet. Counter-bets win on the crisis the shift produces: the generated close that was wrong, the regulator who asks for reproducibility. When that auditor asks "how do you know?", the answer wants to be inside the workbook already, not across the street in a better building.

The precedent for the project's *current* posture is exact, and it is not a Lisp. JavaScript was born a tenant (Netscape, 1995), was specified independently of its host (ECMAScript, 1997), got a second runtime (V8 and Node, 2009), and survived its landlord's death (Netscape, 2003) (*exhibits*). `INTRINSICS.md` is Frazaro's ECMAScript; `SD-18` ("a second-host engine follows the goldens, never leads them") is the rule that makes a second runtime possible (*shelf*). The project has already done the thing that lets an embedded language outlive its building.

### 10.3 What a replacement must build before the first sentence matters

| A WebAssembly competitor builds | The add-in gets free |
|---|---|
| a calc engine: some 500 functions, dynamic arrays, `LAMBDA` and `LET`, the 1900 date bug, 15-digit precision, iterative calculation, volatile functions, structured references, external links | Excel's, bit for bit, including `WHATIF`'s recalculation |
| file fidelity: styles, names, Tables, pivot caches, charts, data validation, conditional formatting | the workbook as it stands |
| a grid: selection, editing, fill, undo | the grid |
| PivotTables, Power Query | the user's existing ones |

| the links: a close is forty workbooks linked, and replacing one breaks them | the links |
| procurement: a new application through finance IT | an `.xlam` and a Store listing |

Every row of the left column is a commodity, and every one is getting cheaper (IronCalc is an open calc engine in Rust that compiles to WebAssembly; open grids exist) (*exhibit*). None of it is the moat. The moat is what the right column leaves out: the grammar, the proofs, the engines, the message catalogue, the register, and the Imitation corpus to come, all host-free *by check* today: `check_translate_purity` (`PORT.2`), `VLA_Browser.bas` (`PORT.1`), `INTRINSICS.md` (`PORT.3`), `SD-1` ("VBA is a backend, not the language"), the head-table fork that makes a second backend a peer, and the two backends already run under `check_backend_parity` (*shelf*). A competitor spends years on the left column before the first sentence matters; the project spends them on sentences, and buys the left column later, when it costs less and the rows it needs are known.

### 10.4 The landlord, priced, and the two costs the shelf did not price

`SUBSTRATE.md` read the record: one eviction attempt (Mac, 2008), lost in one release cycle; one death by default (XLM, 2021, on a twenty-nine-year fuse with the successor installed the whole time); otherwise quarantine. The premise that leaks is "macros permitted", and the watch-list's signal #3 is the fuse (*shelf*). That half is settled.

What that survey did not price are two costs that are the honest argument for a second engine, and neither is the landlord:

1. **VBA's drag on velocity.** The traps, a corpus that met "Procedure too large", an IDE unchanged since 1998, no package manager, no debugger worth the name. The cost compounds, and every item pays it. (*recommendation*: price it per item, as the velocity baseline does.)
2. **The test loop runs through the owner's hands.** The suite cannot run outside Excel, so CI runs static checks and the owner runs the passes; every live pass is an hour of the owner's. The first user of a host-free engine is the test harness. That is not an ark built from a forecast (`SUBSTRATE.md` H.3's objection); it is a cost paid today, measurable in hours per item, and it belongs beside the port's price when the port is weighed. (*recommendation*)

On a landlord whose attention is on language models: `SUBSTRATE.md`'s reading of Python in Excel is that the landlord's successor languages run in *his* cloud (*shelf*). His frontier is there. Frazaro's is on the user's machine, and his choice is what makes the wall a product rather than a preference. One does not leave the city because the mayor took up astrology; one sells the almanac, in the city.

### 10.5 The reframe: a spreadsheet that makes spreadsheets

The third answer, and it dissolves the dilemma rather than picking a side. The shelf had walked up to it three times (*shelf*):

- `SD-1`: "VBA is a backend, not the language." A compiler's constitution.
- `OUTPUT-EXCEPTION.md`, modelled on the GCC Runtime Library Exception. Add-ins do not need output exceptions; compilers do. The licence already knows what the product is.
- `CONTEMPLATIONS.md`, Contemplation 4: "the real product of the fat client is a file format"; "six platforms is two engines"; and `F.7`, the formula dialect, "the backend that still ships even on web Excel, where VBA never had a tenancy at all."

Put together: Frazaro is a compiler from sentences to spreadsheets. Its first target is Excel-with-VBA. Its second is the formula layer, and this is what makes the reframe more than positioning. What Stage 1 and Stage 2 compile to with no macro, no add-in, in any host:

| Sentence | Compiles to |
|---|---|
| `No cell in column G of sheet Invoices should contain "Unpaid".` | a `COUNTIFS` formula in column C, with conditional formatting for green, yellow and red |
| `Cell B9 of sheet Summary must equal cell C4 of sheet GL.` | `=IF(Summary!B9=GL!C4, ...)` |
| `An invoice is overdue when its Due Date is before today and its Status is "Unpaid".` | a `LAMBDA` (Excel and Sheets both have it) |
| `How many invoices are overdue?` | `COUNTIFS` over the Table |
| `Which customers owe more than 10,000?` | `FILTER`, spilling rightward from column C |
| `Every row of Table Bank should match a row of Table Ledger on Amount and Date.` | an anti-join by `COUNTIFS`, the unmatched rows named |
| `Which formula columns contain a typed-over constant?` | `ISFORMULA` over the used range |

A generated Controls sheet runs with nothing installed, in Excel, in Sheets, in Numbers (which imports `.xlsx`) and in Calc (which opens `.xlsx` and `.ods`), on a machine where macros are blocked by policy. That sidesteps `SUBSTRATE.md`'s active leak entirely: the Butlerian spreadsheet delivered as a file the landlord is bound to open. (*engineering*, in the file's first legend; `F.7` is `~days` on the roadmap with two latent bugs already found: `(- x)` losing its sign, and an inner quote never doubled.)

What cannot be a formula stays in the engine: closure (DATALOG's recursion), PROLOG, OPTIMIZE, ABDUCE, DIFF across files, cause, provenance, why. But those are *readers* of workbooks, and reading a file needs no host: `.xlsx` is XML in a zip, formulas are text, values are cached. `REFLECT` over a file is cheaper than `REFLECT` over COM, and it is the half a port reuses unchanged. So the thing that generates workbooks also reads them back and answers the why-questions over the file. The one engine that needs a calculation is `WHATIF` and `GOAL`, and it stays on the host through the add-in, or runs on an open engine for the subset it covers and refuses by name beyond it. The project never writes a calc engine. (*recommendation*)

What the reframe costs, honestly:

1. **Hand-edited artifacts.** Finance edits workbooks. A generator that owns the file (LaTeX's rule: regenerate, hand edits lost) will not be accepted for models. The resolution is to generate *sheets*, not workbooks: the Controls sheet, the Q&A sheet, the Definitions sheet as generated sheets inside the user's workbook, owned by the generator, the model untouched. The Frazaro tab already is this; the reframe names it.
2. **Batch against live.** A generator answers when run. The close is batch anyway (files, month-end), and the formula-compiled questions stay live in any host, so the Q&A sheet keeps its column C.
3. **Cloud hosts invert the sentence.** Apps Script runs on Google's servers. Contemplation 4 proposed the `SD-13` amendment in its own append-only style ("no *additional* network: the procedure runs where the data already lives") and said it should be written before the port rather than discovered after (*shelf*). That is the owner's to write.
4. **The generated artifact must be provably the sentence.** The goldens discipline extends to generated sheets, byte for byte, in `EDITION-PARITY`'s shape; `SD-18` already says the second host follows the goldens (*shelf*).

And the thesis in the joke: a spreadsheet that makes spreadsheets is to spreadsheets what Lisp is to programs. The maker and the made are in the same medium: the `.xlam` is a workbook, the Frazaro tab is a sheet of sentences, the output is a workbook. Homoiconic at the file level. McCarthy would recognize it, and so would Bricklin.

### 10.6 The order of work, with the trigger for each (*recommendation* throughout)

1. **Now, in words, with no code: reposition.** Frazaro is a compiler from sentences to spreadsheets: first target Excel-with-VBA, second the formula layer of every host, third JavaScript (Office Scripts, Apps Script, the browser). `SD-1` already says it; `README.md` and `VENTURE.md` should say it in the generator's words. This answers an investor's platform question with nothing built: *targets, not landlords; the file formats are ISO standards; the engines and the corpus are host-free by check.*
2. **Stay the add-in for the first customers.** The sentences come from there (`SD-7`), the Imitation Game needs a live host, and the installed models are `REFLECT`'s data. `AXM.1` stays next and nothing here moves it.
3. **`F.7`, the formula dialect, as the first generator backend**, pulled forward the day a user needs a Controls sheet that must run where macros are blocked. The first generated artifact is Stage 1's Controls sheet as formulas.
4. **The file reader before the client.** When `AXM.1` measures `REFLECT`, consider measuring it over the saved file as well as over COM; the file path is the half a port reuses, and the number decides whether the client's reader is cheap.
5. **The host-free engine when one of three fires:** the watch-list's signal #3; a customer on Sheets or on macro-blocked Excel; or the velocity case, the suite running in CI instead of the owner's hands, priced in hours. `SD-18` governs it: it follows the goldens.
6. **Never write a calc engine.** Formulas go to the host; the reader takes cached values; `WHATIF` stays where recalculation already is.

### 10.7 One sentence for the deck

*Frazaro is a compiler from plain sentences to spreadsheets, with a proof for every sentence; its first target is Excel, because that is where the close is, and its output is a file every spreadsheet on earth is built to open.*

The owner never signed a lease. The owner wrote a compiler whose first target happens to be the building everyone is in. The question was never tenant or competitor; it was whether the source language survives the host, and `PORT.1`, `PORT.2` and `PORT.3` are the three preparations that say it does.

## 11. What Frazaro is

*The owner's last question of the sitting, asked for plain English: with Frazaro as a GCC-type generator of spreadsheets for other landlords, a middleware between machine and mind, what is the best entry point? A VS Code-like WebAssembly IDE where sentences are typed and target files come out? An IDE? A controlled-language specification? In one question: what is Frazaro going to become? The answer is kept in the register it was asked in.*

### 11.1 The one-sentence answer

**Frazaro is a language.** A fixed list of English sentences, each with exactly one meaning, and a proof that each one does what it says. Everything else built or imagined, the add-in, the generator, a web page, is a *door* into that language. The doors can change. The language is the thing.

### 11.2 The four layers, in order of how much they matter

1. **The sentences and their meanings.** `No cell in column G should contain "Unpaid".` means one thing, forever (`SD-4`). This layer is a *specification*: a list a stranger could read and implement. "A controlled-language specification" is the right name for it, and it is the layer worth the most.
2. **The things that answer.** `SQL`, `DATALOG`, `PROLOG`, `OPTIMIZE`, and the engines the Singularity adds. They are what make a spreadsheet able to answer a question instead of only holding numbers.
3. **The translators.** Sentence in, spreadsheet out. Today the output is VBA inside Excel; next it can be plain formulas, which every spreadsheet program understands; later JavaScript. This layer is the project's GCC: one input, several outputs, held to one meaning by the goldens.
4. **The places one types.** The Frazaro tab in Excel. The CLI window. A text file and a command. A web page. These are the doors. None of them is Frazaro, any more than Notepad is HTML.

So: an IDE is layer 4, a door. A generator is layer 3, an engine. The specification is layer 1, the asset. All four are being built; the project *owns* layer 1, and the other three exist to carry it.

### 11.3 The HTML, CSS and JavaScript instinct, mapped

It fits, because the sentences already come in three kinds:

- **The tables are the HTML.** What is there.
- **Definitions and controls are the CSS.** What should be true of what is there, declared once, applied everywhere, never written as steps. `An invoice is overdue when ...` is a style rule for numbers.
- **Directives are the JavaScript.** Do this: `Delete blank rows in A1:F200.`
- **The spreadsheet program is the browser.** Excel, Sheets, Numbers and Calc each render the same source.
- **The Frazaro tab is "view source".** Any workbook can show the sentences that made it.

The web won because the person *reading* a page needs nothing but a browser, and the person *writing* one needs only a text box. That is the generator model exactly: the writer needs Frazaro; the reader needs nothing but the spreadsheet program already on the machine.

### 11.4 The entry point: one room, three doors

There is not one best door. There is one best *room*, and it should look the same through every door: **a sentence in column B, its result in column C.** The owner already decided this, and it is right, because the language is sentences and not code, so the editor is a sheet and not VS Code.

- **In Excel**, the room is the Frazaro tab. That door is open today, and it is the right one for the first users, whose problem is already in the workbook.
- **On the web**, the room is the same two columns in a browser, with one more button: *Download as .xlsx*. Three sentences typed, one workbook that runs anywhere with nothing installed. This door is for everyone who cannot or will not install anything: the evaluator, the Sheets user, the locked-down laptop, the investor with ten seconds. `VLA_Browser.bas` and `INTRINSICS.md` were built for precisely this page, which the shelf calls the "Google Translate" page (*shelf*).
- **On the command line**, the room is a text file of sentences and `frazaro build controls.txt`. This door is for IT, for CI, and for anyone generating a hundred workbooks.

The web door matters more than it looks, and LaTeX is the proof: TeX is forty years old and painful to install, and Overleaf (WriteLaTeX, 2012), a web page where one types and sees the PDF, became the door through which a generation met the language (*exhibit*). The web door is not a VS Code-like IDE. It is two columns and a download button. (*recommendation*: the shape; the timing is `SD-7`'s, per `SUBSTRATE.md` H.3 and Contemplation 4, and the fundraising calendar's.)

### 11.5 What it becomes if it works

SQL is the closest thing in the world to what is being described. SQL began as the Structured *English* Query Language in 1974 (*exhibit*). It is a sentence language with one meaning per sentence; it is a published standard; many companies built engines that run it; one can type it at a console, in an IDE, inside a program, or in a web box. Nobody says SQL is an IDE. SQL is a language, and the products are engines and doors.

That is the path. **Now**, Frazaro is a language with one engine and one door. **If it works**, it becomes a standard: a published list of sentences and meanings that other people can implement, with the proofs as the test an implementation has to pass. `GRAMMAR_SINCE.md` and `SD-4` are the first pages of that standard, and the goldens are already its conformance test; the project has been writing a standard for a year without calling it one (*shelf*).

In one question and one answer. *What is Frazaro going to become?* **The SQL of spreadsheets: a sentence language that any spreadsheet can run, that proves every sentence, and that the reader never has to install.** The add-in is the first engine. The generator is the second. The web page is the front door.

**The contract.** The core is a pure function over bytes: sentences and phrasebook text in; VLA text, VBA text, formulas, messages, or a whole workbook's bytes out; workbook bytes in, relations and answers out. No clock, no randomness, no threads, no file system, no socket. On the wasm target that is a property of the binary rather than a policy: the import section is empty, which `wasm-objdump -x core.wasm` shows in one line, and a `check_core_imports.ps1` in house style pins the count at zero. `SD-13` stops being a promise an IT reviewer takes on faith and becomes a fact verified on the artifact; `IT_REVIEW.md` gains the sentence. (*recommendation*)

**Why Rust.** One crate builds the native CLI and the wasm module from the same source; no runtime, no garbage collector, no pauses inside a budget; `f64` is VBA's `Double` exactly (IEEE 754); the file crates exist (`calamine` reads `.xlsx` and `.ods` with formula text; `rust_xlsxwriter` writes formulas, Tables, conditional formats and defined names; `zip` and `quick-xml` beneath; an ODF writer in the ecosystem), `wasm-bindgen` for the page and `wasmtime` for every other embedding (*exhibits*). The compiler's strictness is the house style turned into a tool: the ratchets written in PowerShell become types. TypeScript is the alternative; it gets the web door faster and loses the provable empty-import wall and the native CLI. The JavaScript hosts (Office Scripts, Apps Script) never need the core to run *inside* them, because the core *emits* JavaScript for them, which Contemplation 4 already named the cheap route (*shelf*).

**What the core does not contain, on purpose.** A calc engine: formulas go to the host, which computes them on open (`fullCalcOnLoad` in the workbook's `calcPr` forces it); the reader takes cached values; `WHATIF` and `GOAL` stay where recalculation already lives, the add-in, or run later on an open engine behind a declared subset that refuses by name. A grid: the page is two columns, and a full grid is a commodity to adopt if a door ever needs one, never to build.

### 12.3 The six VBA-isms, and the data both implementations read

`INTRINSICS.md` is the whole list: ASCII case folding (`Fold`), numeric literals through `Val` and `Str$`, 1-based strings except `Split`, `Collection` semantics, binary comparison, and `Err.Raise` at the seam. The second is where the port *improves* on the reference by construction: the core owns its number formatting, locale-free, so `DATALOG.17`'s class of bug (a number spelled by `CStr` on a comma-decimal Windows) cannot occur in it.

Everything that defines the language is already data the core reads unchanged: `scripts/prelude.vla`, `scripts/polyglotta/*.vla`, the head table's catalogue, `VLA_Messages.bas`'s catalogue (exported once to a data file, then owned there), `scripts/proofs/datalog.vla`, `instructions.txt` and its goldens. That is the meaningful sense in which Frazaro is self-hosted already: the grammar is a file, and the port is a second interpreter of the same file. It is also why two implementations can be held to one meaning.

### 12.6 The spreadsheet that answers questions about itself

Every workbook the generator builds carries four things, and none needs Frazaro installed to work:

1. **View source.** A `Frazaro` sheet with the sentences in column B, exactly as written.
2. **Live answers.** The Controls, Q&A and Definitions sheets as native formulas (`COUNTIFS`, `FILTER`, `LAMBDA`, conditional formatting); column C stays live in any host, with no add-in, on a machine where macros are blocked. Questions only an engine can answer (closure, why) are written as values with a note beside them: `computed at build from saved values; rebuild to refresh`. Static answers are labelled static, always.
3. **Its own structure, as data.** Two hidden Tables the build writes: `_frazaro_refers(from, to)`, the dependency graph at build time, and `_frazaro_cells(addr, kind)` with `kind` in input, formula, plug. The workbook answers `Which inputs feed B9?` with a `FILTER` over its own Table: no engine at run time; the spreadsheet carries its own reflection.
4. **A build stamp, and reproducible bytes.** A defined name `Frazaro.Build` holding the core's version and the SHA-256 of the sentences; the writer is deterministic, so the same sentences and the same core give the same bytes on every machine. `frazaro rebuild out.xlsx` reads the sentences back out of the `Frazaro` sheet, builds again, and compares: `This workbook was built from these 23 sentences by core 0.9.0: yes.` A sign-off's hash becomes something a second person can re-derive, which is the record's first honest line.

The loop closes: sentences make a workbook; the workbook carries its sentences; the sentences rebuild the workbook; the bytes match. A spreadsheet that holds its own source, proves its own build, and answers questions about its own structure with its own formulas. That is section 3's Rosetta workbook with the fiction removed: it carries the source and the hash of the compiler, which is everything short of carrying the compiler.

### 12.9 The module system: libraries of sentences

*Appended 2026-10-01, the owner's feature, in the owner's example: `Use pie_chart.txt with header: "Q1 Expenses", colors: company_palette.txt, width: 100%, and height: 100%.` imports a file of English sentences written by another user, with slots such as `{width}` that take the arguments of the calling sentence, "to mimic libraries in traditional source code, so that spreadsheets can be composed with shorter and shorter files as time passes and sentences abstract away as much boilerplate typing as possible." The owner asked that it be featured prominently in the roadmap; this is where the re-sort of section 10.6 takes it from. Marks as in section 10; nothing is minted.*

**What it is, in the house's terms.** A paragraph-level macro: a sentence that expands into sentences of the published grammar. The phrasebook already has the sentence-level kind (a rule with a VLA body, which carries power and so passes `SEC.2`'s consent gate); `SPITBALLS.md`'s sitting of 2026-09-19 proposed the English-to-English arrow, "brevity, never power, so no consent prompt", with the expansion visible so that an auditor never meets a private word (*shelf*). A library is that arrow at the file level: it adds no sentence the grammar lacks and no power the reader has not already granted; it adds *paragraphs*. In Lisp's terms it is `defmacro` for paragraphs, and the expansion the build writes out is `macroexpand-1` for auditors. In TeX's terms it is `\usepackage[header=Q1 Expenses,width=100%]{pie_chart}`, and CTAN is the precedent for where libraries live: a repository the user clones, which the compiler never fetches from (*exhibit*). The corpus program `scripts/instructions.txt` is already a build script; the feature lets build scripts use build scripts.

**The call.** `Use <file> with <slot>: <value>, <slot>: <value>, and <slot>: <value>.` Slots are named, never positional, so a library can add a slot with a default without breaking a single caller, which is what `SD-4` demands of any spelling that will be used forever (*recommendation*). An optional label names the use: `Use pie_chart.txt as "Expenses" with ...`, and the label is the hygiene prefix below. A value is a text, an expression (a percentage is one), a cell, a range, a column, a sheet, a Table, or another file.

**The library.** A file of sentences in the published grammar, nothing else, with one declaration sentence at the top in the same grammar: `This file takes header: text, colors: a file, width: a percentage (100% unless given), and height: a percentage (100% unless given).` Its slots are written the phrasebook's own way, `{header:text}`, `{width:expr}`, `{colors:file}`, with the grammar's own slot types plus `file`. A text slot stands where a literal stands (`titled {header}`, never `titled "{header}"`). A library may carry `test:` lines, as a rule does, so a library ships with its proof. A file containing any raw VLA or VBA is not a library but a phrasebook, and takes `SEC.2`'s gate; the parser refuses a library that holds anything but sentences, in words.

**Substitution is structural, not textual.** Scheme's `syntax-rules`, not C's `#define` (*exhibit*). Each library sentence is parsed once with its slots as typed placeholders; the call's values are parsed as typed forms and bound into the placeholders. A text value that happens to contain a full stop cannot become two sentences; a range value cannot become a column. This is the difference between a module system and an injection hazard, and it is the one choice here that cannot be changed later.

**Exactly the slots.** A `Use` supplies every declared slot that has no default and no slot the file does not declare; `check_message_slots` already holds refusals to that discipline, and a library gets the same check (*shelf*). The refusal names the library file and its line *and* the `Use` row that imported it: `pie_chart.txt, sentence 3, used at row 7: no slot named colour; the file takes colors.`

**Hygiene without `GENSYM`.** A name a library creates (a chart's name, a defined name, a helper range) is prefixed by the use's label, deterministically and derivably, under a prefix Check refuses to a program's own names, which is the house rule for generated names (*shelf*). Two unlabelled uses of the same library that would create the same name are refused in words, naming the choice: `two uses of pie_chart.txt both create the name Chart; give each a label with as.` Never a silent fresh symbol.

**Nesting, with a budget and no recursion.** A library may `Use` a library (`pie_chart.txt` uses `{colors}`, and a palette file is a library of `Define` sentences, which is `SPITBALLS.md`'s palette-as-header-file). Depth is budgeted and the budget is a number in the refusal; a cycle is refused by name; a file cannot use itself. The expansion is therefore finite by construction: a module system, not a programming language inside the language, which keeps "brevity, never power" true.

**Visible expansion, and the record.** The build writes the expanded sentences beneath the `Use` line in the output's `Frazaro` sheet, each marked with its source (`from pie_chart.txt, sentence 3`), so view-source (section 12.6) shows the real program and the short one. Check runs on the expansion. The build stamp hashes the main script and every file used, so `frazaro rebuild` verifies the whole composition, and a library that changed under a workbook is detected rather than suspected.

**Provenance and the wall.** A library is a program file: `SEC.8`'s provenance gate applies to it exactly as to a program in a cell. Paths are local only: beside the build script, under `%APPDATA%\Frazaro` (which `VLA_Loader` already owns), or in a registry the user cloned; `Delta/Frazaro-Phrasebooks/`, a skeleton today, is the natural registry (*shelf*). Nothing is fetched (`SD-13`). A library written in the intersection profile runs through every door; one that uses an Excel-only sentence refuses by name on the other hosts, as Contemplation 4 asked (*shelf*).

**Where it sits, and why it is prominent.** It is the *linker* of the compiler of section 12, and it is the one feature that serves both roads at once. On the generator road it is what makes files shorter over time. On the Analyst ex Machina road it is the Controls sheet as a paragraph: `Use month_end_controls.txt with ledger: Table GL, bank: Table Bank, and tolerance: 10%.` is the twenty-two controls of `SINGULARITY.md` section 4, written once by a controller and used by every subsidiary. The first libraries name themselves: that controls paragraph, the palettes, and the onboarding samples that `tools/build_examples.ps1` builds today. And it does not wait for the port: it is a grammar sentence, a loader path, a Check discipline and an expansion written to the Frazaro tab, all of which the VBA host can ship first under `SD-18`, with the goldens carrying it to the core. In the re-sort it belongs on the generator line immediately after the writer, and it is on the AXM line as the form the Controls sheet template takes. (*recommendation*)
